Training

How to Train New Staff with SOPs (Without Spending a Week on It)

Training a new starter usually means a week of shadowing, repeated explanations, and hoping nothing important was missed. With SOPs, the same training becomes a short, repeatable routine: read the procedure, watch it done once, try it with support, and get checked. The new person learns faster, and your best staff get their time back.

Start before day one

Good training starts before the new person arrives. Decide which SOPs they need in their first week and put them in order, simplest first. Prepare their login, uniform, tools, and workspace in advance, and name one person as their go-to for questions. When the basics are ready, the first day can focus on learning the work instead of waiting around for passwords and paperwork. A short welcome note with the first few SOPs to read gives a new starter something useful to do from the first hour.

Use the read, watch, try, check routine

For each task, follow the same four steps. First, the new starter reads the SOP so they know the steps and the order. Then they watch an experienced colleague do the task once, with the SOP open so they can match each step to what they see. Next, they try it themselves while the colleague watches and helps. Finally, someone checks the result against the SOP and signs it off. This routine works for almost any job, from taking an order to cleaning a room, and it gives both sides a clear sense of progress.

Train in small pieces, in the right order

Do not hand a new starter the whole manual on day one. Break training into short sessions focused on one task at a time, starting with the jobs they will do most often. Short sessions stick better than long ones, and finishing one task well builds confidence for the next. Space the sessions across the first week or two rather than cramming them into a single day. Between sessions, let the new starter practise the tasks they have already learned, so the knowledge has time to settle before new material arrives.

Let the SOP do the repeating

The biggest time saving comes from letting the written procedure answer the repeated questions. When a new starter asks how something is done, point them to the SOP first and talk through it together, rather than explaining from scratch each time. If the SOP does not answer their question, that is useful feedback: update the SOP so the next person does not need to ask either. Over time, your SOPs get better with every new starter, and training gets faster with every new starter too.

Check understanding, not just attendance

Finishing a training session is not the same as being able to do the job. After each task, ask the new starter to explain the steps back in their own words, or to do the task while you watch without prompting. A simple sign-off sheet listing each SOP and the date it was checked gives you a clear record of what the person can do unsupervised. It also protects the business: if a mistake happens later, you can see whether the training was completed or whether a step was missed.

Frequently asked questions

How long should training take with SOPs?

It depends on the job, but most routine tasks can be trained in a single short session each. A new starter can often work with light supervision within their first few days, instead of needing a full week of shadowing.

What if the new starter has experience already?

Still walk through your SOPs with them. Experienced people bring good habits, but they also bring habits from other workplaces. Your SOPs show them how your business does it.

Who should train the new starter?

Your best and most patient team member, not necessarily the fastest. A good trainer follows the SOP, explains why steps matter, and checks the work kindly.

What if an SOP turns out to be wrong during training?

Fix it straight away and tell the team. Training is one of the best ways to find errors in SOPs, because a new person follows exactly what is written.
