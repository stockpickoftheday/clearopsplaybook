10 SOPs Every Small Business Needs First

You do not need fifty SOPs to run a well-organized business. You need the right ten, covering the jobs that happen every day and the ones that cause real trouble when they go wrong. Write these first, and the rest can follow as your business grows.

Daily routines: opening and closing

Opening sets the tone for the whole day, and closing protects your money, stock, and premises overnight. These two SOPs are the foundation of the list:

  • Opening up: unlocking and checking the premises, turning on equipment, checking messages and bookings, counting the till or float, and making sure the space is clean and ready before the first customer arrives.
  • Closing down: cashing up and recording the day's takings, securing cash and valuables, switching off equipment, locking up, and setting any alarm.

When opening and closing are written down, any team member can start or end the day properly, and nothing important gets skipped in a rush or done two different ways by two different people.

Money: payments, refunds, and ordering

Most small-business mistakes involve money, so three of the ten SOPs cover it. A payment SOP covers each method you accept, how to handle a declined card, how to give change, and what to do when the till or card machine stops working. A refunds and returns SOP sets out who may approve a refund, what proof the customer needs, how the refund is given, and how it is recorded — so staff can settle most returns calmly on the spot. An ordering SOP covers checking stock levels, who places orders, which suppliers to use, how to check deliveries, and where to store what arrives, which prevents both empty shelves and piles of stock nobody needs.

Customers: complaints and service

A complaint handled well can keep a customer; a complaint handled badly can lose several. Your customer complaints SOP should cover listening without arguing, apologizing for the experience, offering a fair fix within clear limits, and recording what happened so the same problem can be prevented. It should also say when to involve the owner or manager. Alongside it, a simple customer service SOP — how to greet people, take orders or bookings, and follow up — keeps the everyday experience consistent no matter who is serving.

Safety and hygiene

Two SOPs protect people and premises. A cleaning and hygiene SOP lists what to clean, how often, with which products, and who is responsible for each area; it matters most in food, childcare, and health-related businesses, where hygiene is part of keeping customers safe. A health and safety SOP covers the hazards in your business, the protective gear or precautions to use, where the first aid kit and fire equipment are, and what to do when someone is hurt or something goes wrong. Keep both specific to your premises, and make sure every new starter reads them on day one.

People and privacy: onboarding and customer data

The last two SOPs cover the people side of the business. A hiring and onboarding SOP covers what to prepare before a new starter arrives, what to show them on day one, which SOPs they need to read first, and who checks their work in the early weeks — so every new person gets the same fair start. A customer data and privacy SOP covers what data you collect, where it is stored, who may see it, how long to keep it, and what to do if someone asks for their details. Clear rules here protect your customers and protect your business.

Frequently asked questions

In what order should I write these ten?

Start with the one causing the most problems right now — usually opening and closing, payments, or complaints. Early wins build momentum, and the team will start asking for the next ones.

Do I need all ten before I can start using SOPs?

No. Even three or four solid SOPs make a visible difference. Add the rest one at a time as you have time, and review them as your business changes.

Can one SOP cover more than one of these jobs?

It is better to keep them separate. Short, single-task SOPs are easier to find, easier to follow, and easier to update than one long document that tries to cover everything.

Where can I get these SOPs without writing them all myself?

A ready-made kit covers the common small-business SOPs in a consistent format, so you can adapt them to your business instead of writing each one from a blank page.
