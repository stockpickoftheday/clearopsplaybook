# Restaurant Operations Manual
> Ten ready-to-use procedures for a small restaurant. Written out in full — no blanks to fill in.

## What's inside
- SOP 01 — Opening the restaurant
- SOP 02 — Closing the restaurant
- SOP 03 — Greeting and seating guests
- SOP 04 — Taking orders and allergy checks
- SOP 05 — Food safety — fridge and temperature checks
- SOP 06 — Handwashing and hygiene
- SOP 07 — Handling a complaint
- SOP 08 — Cash and card handling
- SOP 09 — Shift handover
- SOP 10 — Cleaning schedule and deep clean

This manual is written for The Copper Kettle Bistro, a 40-seat neighbourhood bistro with 8 staff. Every procedure names the person who does it, the exact steps in order, and what to do when something goes wrong. Use it as it stands, or change the names, times and supplier details to match your own restaurant.

## SOP 01 — Opening the restaurant
**Purpose:** To open on time, safely and ready for the first guest, with the kitchen, dining room and till all checked before service.
**Scope:** Every opening shift, seven days a week. Covers the building check, kitchen start-up, dining room set-up and the till.
**Who does it:** The opening manager (Mara) and the opening cook (Tom). The manager unlocks the building and checks the dining room and till. The cook starts the kitchen.
### Steps
1. Arrive 90 minutes before the first booking. Unlock the front door, switch off the alarm with your own code, and switch on the lights, heating and ventilation.
2. Walk the whole building first. Check for leaks, broken glass, gas or smoke smells, and clear back door and fire exits. If anything is wrong, do not start service — call the manager on duty.
3. Check the fridges and freezers first. Fridges must read 0 to 5 °C, freezers minus 18 °C or colder. Write the readings on the temperature log (see SOP 05). Move anything out of temperature to the quarantine shelf and tell the head chef.
4. Turn on the ovens, grill and fryers so they reach cooking temperature before prep starts. Run hot water at every sink until it is steaming, and check the handwash sinks have soap, paper towels and hot water.
5. Set up the dining room: wipe tables, check chairs are steady, lay cutlery and glasses, fill salt, pepper and oil, and put out fresh menus. Check the toilets are clean and stocked.
6. Check the till. Count the $150 float, sign the float sheet, and check the card machine is charged, on and printing.
7. Work through the opening checklist on the wall by the pass. Every box must be ticked and initialled before the doors open. If a box cannot be ticked, write why and tell the manager.
8. Fifteen minutes before opening, hold a 5-minute team huddle. Read out the bookings, large parties, soup and specials, and any 86'd items (things we have run out of). Confirm who is on each section.
9. Unlock the front door at opening time. The first server stands at the door to greet the first guests.
**Quality check:** The manager walks the dining room and kitchen 10 minutes before opening and signs the opening checklist. Nothing opens until the checklist is complete.
**If it goes wrong:** If a fridge is out of temperature, quarantine the stock and call the manager — do not serve food from it. If the till or card machine fails, take cash only and put a sign at the door. If a staff member is late, the manager covers their section and notes it on the rota.
**Records:** Opening checklist (daily, kept for 3 months), fridge and freezer temperature log (daily, kept for 3 months), float sheet (daily, kept with the till records).

## SOP 02 — Closing the restaurant
**Purpose:** To close safely, leave the kitchen and dining room clean and secure, and make sure the money is counted and the building locked.
**Scope:** Every closing shift. Covers the last guests, kitchen shut-down, cleaning, cashing up and locking the building.
**Who does it:** The closing manager (Mara or the duty manager) and the closing cook (Tom). The last server on duty helps with the dining room.
### Steps
1. At last orders, 30 minutes before closing, tell every table the kitchen is closing and ask if they want a last drink or dessert. Take no new food orders after that.
2. When the last guests leave, lock the front door and turn the sign to closed. Do not let anyone new in after this point.
3. Clear every table, stack chairs on tables where the floor needs mopping, and wipe down all tables, the bar and the till counter.
4. Turn off the fryers, grill and ovens. Leave the extraction fan running for 15 minutes after the last cooking, then switch it off.
5. Put all leftover food away. Cool hot food quickly — spread it in shallow trays and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking. Label everything with the date made and the use-by date. Throw away anything past its use-by date or left at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
6. Clean as you go and finish with the closing clean: wash, rinse and sanitise all work surfaces, sweep and mop the kitchen floor, empty the bins and take the rubbish out to the locked bins outside.
7. Count the till with the manager. Set aside the $150 float for the next day, count the rest, and record the total on the cash-up sheet. Put the takings in the safe and lock it. Card machine totals must match the till report — if not, note the difference and tell the manager before you leave.
8. Do a final walk-through with the closing checklist: gas and water taps off, fridges running, dishwasher empty and off, back door locked, alarm set.
9. The last person out locks the front door, sets the alarm and checks the door is properly shut.
**Quality check:** The closing manager signs the closing checklist and cash-up sheet. The kitchen must pass a visual check — clean surfaces, nothing left out, bins emptied.
**If it goes wrong:** If the safe will not open or the cash does not balance, do not leave — call the manager. If you smell gas, do not switch anything on, open the windows, leave the building and call the gas emergency line. If the alarm will not set, lock up, note it and tell the manager first thing.
**Records:** Closing checklist (daily, kept for 3 months), cash-up sheet (daily, kept for 12 months), food waste log (daily, kept for 3 months).

## SOP 03 — Greeting and seating guests
**Purpose:** To make every guest feel welcome from the moment they walk in, seat them promptly, and set the tone for the meal.
**Scope:** Every guest arrival during service. Covers walk-ins, bookings and waiting guests.
**Who does it:** The host or the first server on duty. During busy service the manager helps with the door.
### Steps
1. Greet every guest within 30 seconds, with eye contact and a smile. Say "Welcome to The Copper Kettle — how can I help you this evening?"
2. Ask if they have a booking. If so, check the bookings book or tablet, confirm the name and party size, and take them to their table. If they are a walk-in, check which tables are free.
3. Seat guests at a table that fits their party. Do not put a party of two at a table for six unless the restaurant is nearly full and the manager agrees. Offer the best table available, not the one nearest the toilets or kitchen door.
4. If a guest asks for a different table, try to move them. If the one they want is booked, say so and offer the next best option. Never argue about a table.
5. Once seated, hand out menus opened at the right page, offer water, and give them their server's name and say you will be right back for drinks. For children, offer a children's menu and a high chair if needed.
6. If there is a wait, be honest. Say "About 20 minutes" and mean it. Take their name and number, offer a drink at the bar, and call as soon as a table is free. Never quote a shorter wait — it backfires.
7. Note special needs on the booking or table ticket — wheelchair access, a quiet corner, a birthday, a pram — and pass them to the server so guests are not asked twice.
8. At the end of the meal, thank guests by name if you know it and say "We hope to see you again soon." If a guest had a problem, tell the manager before they leave, not after.
**Quality check:** The manager watches the door during the first hour and checks no guest waits more than 30 seconds for a greeting. The bookings book is checked each shift for missed notes.
**If it goes wrong:** If a table is double-booked, apologise, offer a free drink at the bar, and seat the guests at the next free table as soon as possible. If a guest dislikes their table, move them if you can and note it for the next host. If a guest arrives very early, offer a drink and seat them early if the table is ready.
**Records:** Bookings book or tablet (daily), wait list with names and phone numbers (daily, shredded at the end of the night), notes on special requests passed to the server.

## SOP 04 — Taking orders and allergy checks
**Purpose:** To take every order accurately, record food allergies and dietary needs correctly, and give the kitchen what it needs to serve safe food.
**Scope:** Every food and drink order, dine-in and takeaway, including all allergy information.
**Who does it:** The server taking the table. The cook on the pass checks the ticket before food leaves the kitchen.
### Steps
1. Reach the table within 5 minutes of seating and offer drinks first. Repeat the order back before you walk away — this catches mistakes early.
2. Write down or enter each dish exactly as the guest says it, including how meat is cooked and changes such as no onions or dressing on the side. Read the whole order back before you leave.
3. Ask about allergies at every table, every time: "Before you order, does anyone at the table have a food allergy or intolerance we should know about?" Never skip this, even with regulars.
4. If a guest says yes, write the allergen on the ticket in capital letters — for example, "NUT ALLERGY — NO PEANUTS" — and tell the cook on the pass as well. Never rely on memory alone.
5. Check the allergen chart behind the pass for every dish ordered. If a dish contains the allergen, or you are not sure, tell the guest and offer a safe alternative. Never guess — if you cannot confirm a dish is safe, say so.
6. The kitchen keeps a written allergen chart for every dish, covering the 14 main allergens: celery, gluten cereals, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, peanuts, sesame, soya, sulphur dioxide, tree nuts. It is updated the same day any recipe changes.
7. Enter the order on the till and send it to the kitchen. Check the ticket prints at the pass. If a ticket is unclear, the cook must ask — never cook from a guess.
8. For takeaway orders, repeat the order and the allergy question on the phone or at the counter, write the allergy on the bag, and put an allergen note inside.
9. When food is ready, the cook calls the table number. The server checks the plate against the ticket, including any allergy note, before carrying it out.
**Quality check:** The cook on the pass checks every ticket for an allergy note before food goes out. The manager spot-checks two tables a shift to hear the allergy question asked.
**If it goes wrong:** If a guest has an allergic reaction, stop serving them at once, call an ambulance if they show serious signs (trouble breathing, swelling of face or lips), and tell the manager. Keep the dish, ticket and packaging — do not throw anything away. If a wrong dish reaches a table, apologise, take it away, remake it, and tell the kitchen what went wrong.
**Records:** Order tickets (kept for the day), allergen chart (updated when recipes change, kept at the pass), allergy incident notes (kept for 12 months).

## SOP 05 — Food safety — fridge and temperature checks
**Purpose:** To keep all food at safe temperatures from delivery to the plate, and to catch a failing fridge before it makes anyone ill.
**Scope:** All fridges, freezers and cooked food — daily checks, deliveries and cooling.
**Who does it:** The opening cook (Tom) does the morning checks. The cook on shift does the checks at midday and at close. The manager checks the log weekly.
### Steps
1. Check every fridge twice a day, at opening and midday. Fridges must read 0 to 5 °C, freezers minus 18 °C or colder. Use the fridge thermometer, not the door display, and write the reading, time and initials on the temperature log.
2. If a fridge reads above 5 °C, move the food to another working fridge at once. If there is no space, use a cool box with ice. Tell the head chef and manager. Do not serve food above 8 °C for more than 4 hours — throw it away.
3. Check deliveries as they arrive. Cold food must arrive at 5 °C or below, frozen at minus 18 °C or below. Refuse anything warm, damaged or past its date, and note it on the delivery note.
4. Date-label everything that goes into a fridge: the date opened or cooked and the use-by date. Use the oldest stock first — first in, first out. Never put new stock in front of old.
5. Cook food to safe temperatures. Chicken, pork and minced meat must reach 75 °C in the thickest part. Steaks may be served rarer only if the guest asks and the meat is a whole cut seared outside. Reheated food must reach 75 °C throughout. Check with a clean probe thermometer.
6. Serve hot food from the pass quickly. Food held hot must stay above 63 °C. If it has been sitting for more than 2 hours, throw it away.
7. Cool cooked food fast. Spread it in shallow trays no more than 5 cm deep and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking. Never put a large hot pot straight in the fridge — it warms everything else.
8. Clean the probe thermometer before and after every use with hot soapy water and sanitiser, and let it air dry.
**Quality check:** The manager checks and signs the temperature log every Monday. Any gap counts as a missed check and is discussed with the team.
**If it goes wrong:** If a fridge breaks down, move the food, tell the manager and call the repair company the same day. If you are not sure food is safe, throw it away — never serve food you are unsure about. If a guest reports feeling ill after eating here, tell the manager at once and keep that day's food records and temperature log.
**Records:** Fridge and freezer temperature log (twice daily, kept for 3 months), delivery checks (daily, kept for 3 months), probe thermometer calibration check (monthly).

## SOP 06 — Handwashing and hygiene
**Purpose:** To stop germs spreading from hands, clothes and surfaces onto food, and to make good hygiene a habit for everyone.
**Scope:** Every member of staff, every shift. Covers handwashing, clothing, illness and personal hygiene.
**Who does it:** Every member of staff. The manager checks compliance daily and the head chef checks the kitchen team.
### Steps
1. Wash your hands when you arrive, before touching any food, after handling raw meat, after touching bins, after using the toilet, after touching your face or hair, after handling money, and after cleaning. In practice, wash far more often than you think you need to.
2. Wash properly every time: wet hands with warm water, apply liquid soap, rub palms, backs, between fingers and under nails for at least 20 seconds, rinse well, and dry with a paper towel. Use the towel to turn off the tap.
3. Use handwash sinks only for washing hands. Never wash food, equipment or cloths in one, and never wash your hands in a food-prep sink.
4. Wear a clean apron and clean clothes every shift. Tie long hair back, keep nails short and clean, and do not wear nail varnish, false nails or jewellery on hands or wrists, except a plain wedding band. Cover any cut with a bright blue waterproof plaster — blue so it can be spotted if it falls into food.
5. Do not come to work if you have vomiting or diarrhoea. Stay away until you have been free of symptoms for 48 hours. With a cold, cough into your elbow, wash your hands more often, and tell the manager. With a skin infection on your hands, tell the manager before your shift — you may need to be moved off food handling.
6. Never eat over food-prep areas — staff meals are eaten in the break area, not the kitchen. Taste food with a clean spoon, never your fingers, and never use the same spoon twice.
7. Keep personal items — phones, bags, coats — in the staff area, not in the kitchen or on work surfaces. Wash your hands after using your phone.
8. Sneeze or cough away from food, into a tissue or your elbow, then wash your hands.
**Quality check:** The manager does a quick hygiene check at the start of every shift — clean apron, hair tied back, hands washed. The head chef spot-checks handwashing in the kitchen during service.
**If it goes wrong:** If you see someone skip handwashing or handle food with dirty hands, speak up on the spot — "Can you wash your hands before you touch that?" — and tell the manager if it happens again. If a plaster or jewellery is lost in the kitchen, stop, find it, and throw away any food it may have touched.
**Records:** Staff illness log (kept for 12 months), hygiene check notes on the shift log (daily), blue plaster issue log (kept at the first-aid box).

## SOP 07 — Handling a complaint
**Purpose:** To sort out a guest's problem quickly and calmly, turn a bad experience into a good one, and learn from every complaint.
**Scope:** Every guest complaint — about food, service, cleanliness or billing — made in the restaurant, on the phone or online.
**Who does it:** The server who hears it starts it. The manager on duty takes over complaints about food safety, allergies or billing.
### Steps
1. Stop, face the guest and listen. Do not interrupt, argue or explain while they talk. Let them finish, even if you think they are wrong.
2. Show you heard them: "I am sorry this happened — let me sort this out for you." Repeat their words back: "So the steak was overcooked and you have waited 40 minutes. I understand why you are unhappy."
3. Offer a fix straight away: remake a wrong dish or bring something else; a free drink or starter for a long wait. For a serious problem, call the manager — do not handle it alone.
4. Only the manager can offer a discount, free meal or refund. Servers can offer a remake, free drink or free dessert. If the guest asks for more, say "Let me get the manager — they can help with that."
5. Once the guest agrees, act fast — a remade dish should be next out of the kitchen. Check back within 5 minutes to make sure they are happy.
6. If a guest is rude or shouting, stay calm and polite — do not raise your voice. Say "I want to help you — let's find a way to fix this." If they turn threatening, call the manager at once and do not stay alone with them.
7. Write the complaint down before your shift ends: date, table, what went wrong, what was offered, whether the guest was happy. Give it to the manager.
8. The manager reviews every complaint at the weekly team meeting. If the same problem comes up twice, the procedure changes — two complaints about cold food means a tighter pass check.
9. For online or email complaints, the manager replies within 24 hours, apologises, explains what was done, and invites the guest back. Never argue online.
**Quality check:** The manager reads the complaint log weekly and checks every complaint has a fix and follow-up recorded. Food safety and allergy complaints are reviewed the same day.
**If it goes wrong:** If a guest walks out without paying after a complaint, do not chase them — note the table, bill and what happened, and tell the manager. If a guest threatens staff or other guests, call the manager and, if needed, the police. If a complaint is about an allergic reaction, follow SOP 04 — stop service, call an ambulance if needed, and keep food and records.
**Records:** Complaint log (daily, reviewed weekly, kept 12 months), online review replies (kept on file), refund and discount notes on the cash-up sheet.

## SOP 08 — Cash and card handling
**Purpose:** To handle every payment honestly and accurately, keep the till balanced, and protect staff and the business from theft and mistakes.
**Scope:** All cash, card and voucher payments, the till float, cashing up and the safe.
**Who does it:** The server taking payment, and the manager who opens, checks and closes the till.
### Steps
1. The opening manager counts the $150 float at the start of the shift — $100 in notes, $50 in coins — and signs the float sheet. Never use the float to pay suppliers or make change for staff.
2. Take payment at the table or the till, as the guest prefers. Say the total clearly before taking money or a card. Count change back in front of the guest.
3. For card payments, never take the card out of the guest's sight. Bring the machine to the table, let the guest enter their PIN or tap the card, and wait for the receipt. Check the amount matches the bill before handing it over.
4. Enter every payment on the till straight away. Do not keep cash in your apron or pocket — it goes in the till. Never leave the till open between payments.
5. To split a bill, split it on the till before taking payment and give each person a receipt. For a voucher or gift card, check the expiry date and balance, and record the voucher number on the till.
6. Card tips are recorded on the till and shared out weekly under the tips policy on the noticeboard. Cash tips left on the table go in the tips jar — never pocketed.
7. At the end of the shift, the manager counts the till with a second staff member present. Set aside the $150 float, count the rest, and fill in the cash-up sheet. Card totals must match the till report.
8. Put the takings in the safe and lock it. Only the manager and owner hold the safe code. The manager empties the safe and banks the money at least twice a week.
9. If the till is more than $10 over or under, write the difference on the cash-up sheet and tell the manager the same day. Small errors happen, but a pattern is investigated.
**Quality check:** The manager checks the cash-up sheet against the card machine report daily. The owner checks weekly totals and bank deposits.
**If it goes wrong:** If the card machine fails during service, take cash only, put a sign at the till, and call the card machine company. If you suspect a fake note, check it against the guide at the till, do not accept it, and tell the manager. If the till is short by a large amount, do not accuse anyone — record it, tell the manager, and let them investigate.
**Records:** Float sheet, cash-up sheet and card machine reports (daily, kept for 12 months), voucher log (daily).

## SOP 09 — Shift handover
**Purpose:** To pass on everything the next shift needs to know, so nothing is missed, no guest is let down, and the kitchen and dining room run smoothly all day.
**Scope:** Every change of shift — morning to lunch, lunch to dinner, and dinner to close.
**Who does it:** The outgoing manager or head server hands over to the incoming manager or head server. The outgoing cook hands over to the incoming cook.
### Steps
1. Start the handover 15 minutes before the outgoing shift ends. Do it face to face at the pass, not by text or by leaving a note. Both people must be present.
2. Walk through the bookings together. Read out the names, times and party sizes for the next 3 hours, plus any large parties, birthdays, anniversaries or special requests. Point out any tables that are already set or held.
3. Report on the kitchen. The outgoing cook tells the incoming cook what is running low, what is 86'd (out of stock), what is prepped and ready, and anything that needs using up first. Check the fridge labels together so nothing is missed.
4. Report on the dining room. Note any tables that are still eating, any guests waiting for food or the bill, any complaints that are still open, and any guests who need extra care.
5. Hand over the money. The outgoing manager tells the incoming manager the till balance, any large cash payments taken, any refunds or discounts given, and any problems with the card machine.
6. Hand over the building. Report any maintenance problems — a dripping tap, a wobbly table, a light that has gone — and anything the next shift needs to know about deliveries expected or suppliers coming.
7. Write the key points on the handover sheet: bookings, 86'd items, open complaints, maintenance issues and any messages for the next shift. Both people sign it.
8. The incoming manager reads the handover sheet back to the outgoing manager before they leave, to check nothing was misunderstood. The outgoing manager does not leave until the incoming manager is happy.
9. The incoming manager holds a 5-minute huddle with their team, covering the bookings, the 86'd items and any open problems, so the whole team starts with the same information.
**Quality check:** The manager on duty checks the handover sheet at the start of their shift and signs it. A missing or unsigned handover sheet is raised at the next team meeting.
**If it goes wrong:** If the outgoing manager has to leave early in an emergency, they phone the incoming manager, go through the handover on the phone, and write the key points in a message that the incoming manager confirms they have read. If a handover is missed, the incoming manager checks the bookings book, the till and the kitchen themselves before service, and notes the gap.
**Records:** Handover sheet (every shift, kept for 1 month), bookings book, till cash-up notes.

## SOP 10 — Cleaning schedule and deep clean
**Purpose:** To keep the restaurant clean and safe every day, and to make sure easy-to-forget jobs — behind equipment, inside ovens, in the drains — get done on schedule.
**Scope:** All daily, weekly and monthly cleaning in the kitchen, dining room, toilets and outside areas.
**Who does it:** All staff share daily cleaning. Tom owns the kitchen schedule, the manager owns the dining room and toilets, and the whole team does the monthly deep clean together.
### Steps
1. Follow the cleaning schedule on the kitchen wall — every job, how often, who does it, what to use. If a job is on the schedule, it gets done, quiet or busy.
2. Daily jobs, every shift: wipe and sanitise work surfaces; clean the pass and hotplate; sweep and mop all floors; clean and restock toilets; wipe tables, chairs and the bar; empty bins; wash and sanitise cloths.
3. Clean as you go: wipe spills at once, put dirty plates and pans straight in the wash-up area, never on a prep surface. A clean-as-you-go kitchen is faster and safer.
4. Weekly jobs, Monday morning before service: clean inside ovens and grill; defrost and clean freezers if needed; clean canopy filters; descale coffee machine and kettles; wipe dry-store shelves and check dates; clean fridge seals and interiors.
5. Monthly deep clean, first Monday of the month when closed: clean behind all equipment; clean drains and grease trap; clean walls, vents and light covers; clean inside the dishwasher and run an empty hot cycle; check extinguishers and the first-aid box.
6. Use the right chemical: sanitiser for food-contact surfaces, degreaser for floors and canopy, descaler for the coffee machine. Follow the label — never mix chemicals, never mix bleach with anything. Wear gloves; keep chemicals away from food.
7. Label every spray bottle with its contents and fill date. Store chemicals in the locked cupboard under the sink, never above food.
8. Tick each job off and initial it. The head chef checks the kitchen schedule each shift; the manager checks the dining room schedule.
**Quality check:** The head chef checks the kitchen each shift — clean surfaces, clean floor, bins empty. The manager checks the whole schedule weekly and signs it, and signs off the monthly deep clean against its list.
**If it goes wrong:** If a chemical spills on food or a food surface, throw the food away, wash, rinse and sanitise the surface, and let it air dry. If a job is missed, note it and do it the next day — do not just tick it off. If drains or the grease trap smell bad, call maintenance the same day.
**Records:** Cleaning schedule (daily, weekly — 3 months), deep clean checklist (monthly — 12 months), chemical safety sheets (folder by the chemical cupboard).
