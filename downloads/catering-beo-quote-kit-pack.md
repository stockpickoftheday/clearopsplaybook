## SOP 01 — Taking a catering enquiry
**Purpose:** To answer every enquiry quickly, in the same way, so no detail is missed and no good lead is lost because someone forgot to ask about the date, the guest count, or the budget.
**Scope:** This covers every first contact with a possible client, by phone, email, website form, or in person, for weddings, corporate lunches, and private parties. It covers Ember & Oak Catering only.
**Who does it:** The owner or the bookings lead. If anyone else takes the call, they take the details and pass them to the bookings lead the same day.
### Steps
1. Answer within one working day. Thank the person for getting in touch and confirm the event date first, because the date decides everything else.
2. Ask for the client's full name, phone number, and email address, and repeat the email address back to them so it is spelled correctly.
3. Ask what kind of event it is, where it will be held, and what time food should be served. Write down the venue name and address exactly as the client gives it.
4. Ask how many guests they expect, including children, and whether that number is firm or a rough guess. Ask for the lowest and highest number they expect.
5. Ask what style of food they want, such as a sit-down dinner, a buffet, canapés and drinks, or a working lunch, and whether they want drinks, staff, hire items, or just food dropped off.
6. Ask about the budget per person, and about any dietary needs, allergies, or cultural food rules. Note anything that affects the menu.
7. Check the diary while you are still talking to them if you can. Tell them honestly whether the date is free, held for someone else, or already booked.
8. Send a short summary email within 24 hours repeating the date, venue, guest count, and food style, and ask them to reply to confirm or correct it. Save it in the client file.
9. If the date is free, offer a quote or a tasting call, and give a clear date by which the quote will arrive. If the date is not free, say so kindly and offer the nearest free dates.
10. Log the enquiry with the date, client name, event date, guest count, and outcome, even if the client does not go ahead.
**Quality check:** Before you send the summary, read it back against your notes. The date, venue, guest count, and food style must match.
**If it goes wrong:** If you gave the wrong date or price range, call the client straight away, apologise, and send a corrected summary the same day. If two clients want the same date, the first to pay a deposit holds it.
**Records:** Keep the enquiry log, the client's contact details, the summary email, and any notes about the venue, budget, or dietary needs in the client file, under the event date.
## SOP 02 — Writing a quote
**Purpose:** To give the client a clear, honest price they can say yes to, with no hidden extras, so the client knows what they are paying for and the job will make money.
**Scope:** This covers written quotes for weddings, corporate lunches, and private parties. It does not cover the final invoice, which uses the final guest count.
**Who does it:** The owner or the bookings lead. The owner checks any quote over $5,000, or any quote with a menu or venue the team has not done before.
### Steps
1. Start from the enquiry notes. Check the date, venue, guest count, food style, service time, and dietary needs before you price anything.
2. Price the food first. Work out the ingredient cost per person, then add staff, travel, fuel, hire items, and packaging. Add a margin for overheads and profit, and write the figures down.
3. List every item as its own line, with the price per person or the flat price beside it: canapés, courses, drinks, cake cutting, staff, hire, delivery, and setup.
4. State the guest number the quote is based on, for example 80 guests, and what happens if the number changes. The price will be recalculated if the count moves by more than ten guests.
5. State what is included and what is not. Say plainly if the venue, tables, chairs, plates, glasses, or a licence are the client's job, not yours.
6. Give the quote a valid-until date, usually 14 days, and state the deposit to hold the date, usually 50 percent. State when the final guest count and balance are due.
7. Add the terms in plain words: cancellation, what happens if the event is moved, and who pays if the venue charges for extra time or damage.
8. Read the quote once for maths and once for sense. Check that every line adds up, the total matches the lines, and the date and guest count are right.
9. Send the quote by email as a PDF, with a short note saying what happens next and when you need an answer. Save a copy in the client file.
10. Follow up once, three working days after sending it. If the client asks for changes, send a revised quote with a new version number and valid-until date, and keep the old version on file.
**Quality check:** The client should be able to work out the total from the lines without asking you. The owner should be able to see the food cost, staff cost, and margin.
**If it goes wrong:** If you underpriced the job, do not cut corners on the day. Tell the owner, work out the real cost, and talk to the client honestly. If you sent the wrong total, correct it in writing the same day.
**Records:** Keep the quote, every revised version, the cost workings, and the client's reply in the client file, filed by event date and client name.

## SOP 03 — Writing a BEO (banquet event order)
**Purpose:** To turn a confirmed booking into one document that tells the kitchen and floor team exactly what happens and when, so nothing is guessed on the day.
**Scope:** This covers every confirmed event once the deposit is paid. The BEO is the only source of truth; if it is not on it, it does not happen.
**Who does it:** The bookings lead writes it, the owner checks it, and the event manager runs the event from it. Chef and lead server each get a copy.
### Steps
1. Start from the signed quote and final choices. Check date, venue, numbers, timings, menu, and dietary needs.
2. Write event facts first: client, event type, date, venue, guest count, contacts, and day lead.
3. Write the timeline in order, with times for setup, service, speeches, cake, and pack-down.
4. Write the menu in full, with dish numbers and dietary versions. Add drinks and bar times.
5. List staff by role and number, with arrival times, and list equipment and food quantities.
6. Write totals at the bottom: deposit paid, balance due, and extra charges for running over.
7. Send it to the client seven days before the event. Ask for written checks; fix errors and reissue.
8. Print copies for kitchen, floor, and event manager. The manager keeps the master and notes changes.
### Worked example — Wedding BEO, Ember & Oak Catering
- **Clients:** Maya and Daniel Thompson, wedding, 12 June 2027, Old Barn at Willow Creek, 80 guests, Maya 555-014-2287.
- **Timings:** 14:00 setup; 15:30 canapés ready; 16:00 ceremony ends; 16:15 canapés; 17:30 seated; 17:45 starter; 18:30 main; 19:15 dessert, speeches; 20:00 cake; 21:30 snack; 23:00 bar closes, pack-down.
- **Menu:** Salmon blinis, mushroom tartlets, tomato skewers. Tomato soup. Herb chicken with roast potatoes, or mushroom wellington (9). Lemon tart. Late snack: sausage rolls, cheese toasties.
- **Dietary:** 9 vegetarian, 4 gluten-free, 2 dairy-free, 1 nut allergy. Allergy dishes plated separately, labelled.
- **Staffing:** 1 event manager, 2 chefs, 1 kitchen assistant, 6 servers, 2 bartenders. All arrive 14:00, bartenders 15:00.
- **Equipment:** 80 place settings, 10 tables, 80 chairs, 2 portable ovens, 3 chafing dishes, 1 mobile bar, 90 wine glasses, 90 water glasses, linen and napkins.
- **Totals:** Canapés $960; dinner $5,440; snack $640; drinks $2,240; cake $160 = $9,440. Staff $1,850; hire $620; delivery $350 = $2,820. Subtotal $12,260; service 10% $1,226; tax 5% $613. Grand total $14,099. Deposit $7,049.50. Balance due 5 June 2027: $7,049.50.
**Quality check:** Read the BEO as if you have never seen the event. You should be able to run it from the page alone, and it must agree with the quote.
**If it goes wrong:** If the client changes the menu or guest count, issue a new version the same day, tell the chef and event manager, and confirm the new total.
**Records:** Keep the final BEO, every earlier version, the client's approval, and the marked-up master copy in the client file.

## SOP 04 — Menu tasting and final numbers
**Purpose:** To let the client taste the food before the event, settle the menu with confidence, and lock in final guest numbers early enough for the kitchen to order correctly.
**Scope:** This covers tastings for weddings and large private parties, and final numbers for every event. Small corporate lunches with a set menu do not need a tasting.
**Who does it:** The owner or the head chef runs the tasting. The bookings lead records the choices and the final numbers, and tells the kitchen and the suppliers.
### Steps
1. Offer the tasting once the quote is accepted and the deposit is paid. Give the client two or three dates, and confirm the time, the place, and how many people may come, usually the couple plus two guests.
2. Prepare small portions of the dishes the client is choosing between, not full meals. Label each dish clearly and serve them in the order they would be served at the event.
3. Ask the client to score each dish simply, for example like, love, or change, and to say what they would change, such as less salt, more sauce, or a different side dish.
4. Write down every decision before the client leaves: the chosen starter, main, dessert, canapés, drinks, and any dietary versions. Read the list back and ask the client to agree it.
5. Send a written summary of the menu choices within two working days, and ask the client to confirm it by reply. Do not start ordering for the chosen menu until they confirm.
6. Ask for final guest numbers 14 days before the event, and again 7 days before. Explain that the 7-day number is the number the kitchen will cook for, plus a small buffer for last-minute changes.
7. If the numbers go up after the 7-day point, check with the chef whether the kitchen can cope, and tell the client the extra cost before you agree. If the numbers go down, explain that food already ordered cannot be refunded.
8. Update the BEO with the final menu and the final numbers, and send the updated version to the client, the chef, and the event manager.
**Quality check:** The confirmed menu should match the tasting notes exactly. The final guest count on the BEO should match the client's last written number, and the kitchen should have ordered for that number plus the agreed buffer.
**If it goes wrong:** If the client dislikes a dish at the tasting, offer one replacement dish rather than a whole new menu, and taste it again if time allows. If final numbers arrive late, cook for the last confirmed number and tell the client what extra guests can and cannot be fed.
**Records:** Keep the tasting notes, the menu summary and the client's confirmation, and every final-numbers message with its date, in the client file.
## SOP 05 — Staffing an event
**Purpose:** To have the right people, in the right roles, at the right time, so service runs smoothly and no one is left doing two jobs at once.
**Scope:** This covers all paid and casual staff at Ember & Oak Catering events, from the event manager to the kitchen assistant. It does not cover venue staff or staff hired directly by the client.
**Who does it:** The owner or the event manager plans the rota. The event manager briefs the team on the day and is the only person who changes jobs during the event.
### Steps
1. Work out the staff needed from the BEO: the guest count, the service style, the timings, and the venue layout. As a guide, allow one server for every 12 to 15 guests at a sit-down dinner, and one bartender for every 40 guests.
2. Book the team at least 14 days before the event. Confirm the date, the arrival time, the venue address, the finish time, the pay rate, and the dress code in writing.
3. Send every staff member a short brief the day before the event, with the BEO timings, the menu, the dietary notes, and their own job for the night.
4. Check on the day that everyone has arrived on time, is dressed correctly, and has washed their hands and tied back long hair. Give anyone who is late a clear job and note the late arrival.
5. Hold a ten-minute team briefing before guests arrive. Cover the running order, who serves which tables, where the dietary dishes go, where the toilets and fire exits are, and who to ask if something goes wrong.
6. Give the kitchen team and the floor team one contact each: the head chef and the lead server. All questions go through them, so the event manager is free to talk to the client.
7. Plan breaks in advance. Staff working more than six hours get a break and a staff meal, taken in turn so service is never left short.
8. At the end of the night, thank the team, confirm their hours, and note any problems, such as a no-show or a job that took longer than planned, so the next rota can be fixed.
**Quality check:** Before guests arrive, count heads against the rota and check that every table and every job has a named person. If a role is empty, move someone to it before service starts, not during it.
**If it goes wrong:** If someone calls in sick, call the standby list first, then ask a trained team member to cover and simplify service if you must, for example by serving dessert as a buffet. Tell the client only if the change will affect them.
**Records:** Keep the rota, the staff brief, the confirmed hours, and any notes about no-shows, late arrivals, or problems in the event file.

## SOP 06 — Food safety at an event
**Purpose:** To keep food safe from the kitchen to the plate, so guests enjoy the meal and no one becomes ill because food was stored, cooked, or served at the wrong temperature.
**Scope:** This covers all food handled by Ember & Oak Catering at an event, including food cooked off-site, food finished on-site, and leftovers. It applies to every team member who touches food.
**Who does it:** The head chef owns food safety on the day. Every team member follows the chef's instructions, and the event manager checks that the rules are followed at the front of house.
### Steps
1. Before you leave the kitchen, check that cold food is below 5°C and hot food is above 63°C, and that chilled transport boxes and hot boxes are clean and working. Record the temperatures.
2. Keep raw meat, fish, and poultry separate from ready-to-eat food at all times, in separate boxes and on separate boards, and wash your hands after handling raw food.
3. Wash hands often, and always after using the toilet, handling rubbish, touching your face, or handling raw food. Use soap and clean water, and dry hands with a clean towel or paper towels.
4. Cook food right through, and check the core temperature with a clean probe. Poultry and minced meat must reach 75°C at the centre. Record the result for the main dishes.
5. Hold hot food above 63°C and cold food below 5°C during service. Do not leave food sitting at room temperature for more than two hours in total, including the time on the buffet or the pass.
6. Label every dish on the pass, including the dietary versions, and keep allergen dishes apart from other dishes, using clean tongs, spoons, and trays for each one.
7. Taste food only with a clean spoon, never with a finger or a used spoon, and never serve food that has been dropped, touched after cooking, or left uncovered outdoors.
8. At the end of service, chill leftovers quickly in shallow trays if the client asks to keep them, or throw them away. Record what was kept, what was thrown away, and the final temperatures.
**Quality check:** The head chef checks the temperature log, the labels, and the separation of raw and cooked food before service starts, and again halfway through service. Anything out of range is fixed or thrown away.
**If it goes wrong:** If food has been out of temperature for too long, or you are unsure whether it is safe, throw it away. Do not serve it and hope for the best. Tell the event manager, note what was thrown away, and tell the client simply what was changed.
**Records:** Keep the temperature log, the probe readings, the cleaning checks, and any notes about food thrown away in the event file for at least three months.
## SOP 07 — Event setup and service
**Purpose:** To set up the room and the kitchen on time, welcome guests warmly, and serve food and drinks in the right order, at the right temperature, without fuss.
**Scope:** This covers arrival at the venue, setup, guest arrival, food and drink service, and the end of service. It applies to weddings, corporate lunches, and private parties.
**Who does it:** The event manager runs setup and service. The head chef runs the kitchen, the lead server runs the floor, and the bartenders run the bar.
### Steps
1. Arrive at the time on the BEO, usually two hours before guests arrive. Check in with the venue contact, find the kitchen, the storage area, the toilets, and the fire exits, and note any venue rules.
2. Set the tables first, following the client's seating plan if there is one. Check that every place has the right plate, cutlery, glass, napkin, and place card, and that dietary place cards are in the right seats.
3. Set up the bar, the drinks station, and the buffet if there is one. Fill water jugs, chill the wine and soft drinks, and put the glasses where the team can reach them easily.
4. Hold the team briefing, then do a walk-through as a guest would: enter the room, look at the tables, check the lighting and the music level, and fix anything that looks untidy.
5. Welcome guests as they arrive. Offer canapés and drinks, keep glasses topped up, and clear empty glasses and plates promptly so tables never look messy.
6. Serve courses in the order on the BEO, and serve the whole room together as far as possible. Serve dietary dishes first, to the right guest, and say quietly what the dish is when you put it down.
7. Keep an eye on the timing. If speeches run long, hold the food hot and tell the chef, so the main course is still served at its best. Tell the client if a delay will change the running order.
8. At the end of service, clear the tables, offer coffee and cake, and check with the client that they are happy before the team starts to pack down.
**Quality check:** Before guests arrive, the event manager walks the room and checks every table, the bar, and the kitchen pass against the BEO. During service, the lead server checks that every table has been served before the next course starts.
**If it goes wrong:** If a dish runs out, tell the chef at once and offer the nearest alternative to the affected guests. If a guest is unhappy, listen, apologise, and fix what you can, then tell the event manager so it is recorded.
**Records:** Keep the seating plan, the marked-up BEO with the actual timings, and any notes about delays, shortages, or guest complaints in the event file.
## SOP 08 — Pack-down and close
**Purpose:** To leave the venue clean, tidy, and as you found it, to get all hire items and equipment back safely, and to close the event properly so nothing is lost or left behind.
**Scope:** This covers pack-down after service ends, at the venue and back at the kitchen. It applies to every event, including drop-off jobs where the team returns later to collect items.
**Who does it:** The event manager runs pack-down. Every team member helps, and the driver checks the load before the van leaves.
### Steps
1. Wait until the client agrees that service is finished, or until the time on the BEO. Tell the venue contact that pack-down is starting, and agree where rubbish and recycling should go.
2. Clear the tables first, then the bar, then the kitchen. Sort plates, glasses, cutlery, and linen into their boxes as you go, so nothing is broken or lost.
3. Count the hire items against the hire list, and check them for damage or loss. Note any broken or missing item, and tell the event manager before you leave.
4. Pack food and equipment safely. Put chilled food into clean chilled boxes, label leftovers the client is keeping, and throw away food that cannot be kept safely.
5. Clean as you go. Wipe the tables, sweep the floor, clean the kitchen area you used, and take all rubbish to the agreed bins. Leave the toilets and the entrance tidy.
6. Do a final walk-through with the venue contact if they are there. Check the kitchen, the store, the toilets, the car park, and the van, and look for anything left behind.
7. Load the van in order, with fragile items on top and heavy items at the bottom, and strap the load so it cannot move. The driver checks the doors and the lights before leaving.
8. Back at the kitchen, unload the van, put clean hire items away, start the washing, and record the hours, the hire count, and any damage or loss.
**Quality check:** Before the van leaves, the event manager checks the hire count, the rubbish, and the room against the venue checklist. The room should look as it did when the team arrived.
**If it goes wrong:** If a hire item is broken or missing, note it honestly, tell the owner the same night, and contact the hire company the next working day. If something is left at the venue, call the venue the next morning and arrange collection.
**Records:** Keep the hire count, the damage and loss notes, the hours worked, and the venue sign-off, if there is one, in the event file.

## SOP 09 — Invoicing and chasing payment
**Purpose:** To get paid in full and on time for every event, without awkwardness, by invoicing promptly, stating the terms clearly, and following up politely but firmly.
**Scope:** This covers the final invoice, the balance payment, extra charges after the event, and chasing any payment that is late. The deposit is covered in SOP 02.
**Who does it:** The bookings lead sends the invoice. The owner chases any payment that is more than 14 days late, and decides whether to add a late fee or pause future bookings.
### Steps
1. Send the final invoice within two working days of the event, or on the date agreed in the quote, using the final guest count and the final BEO, not the first estimate.
2. Show the deposit already paid, the balance due, and any extra charges as separate lines, with a short reason for each extra, such as extra guests, extra hours, or a broken hire item.
3. State the payment due date clearly, usually 14 days from the invoice date, and give the bank details, the invoice number, and the client's name on the invoice.
4. Check the invoice before you send it. The totals must match the BEO and the quote, the deposit must be deducted once, and the client's name and address must be right.
5. If the balance was due before the event, check it has arrived before the team leaves for the venue. If it has not, call the client that morning and agree payment before service starts.
6. Send one friendly reminder three days before the due date if payment has not arrived, thanking the client for the event and repeating the due date and the bank details.
7. If payment is late, send a short reminder on the due date, then again seven days later, and call the client after 14 days. Stay polite, keep a record of every message, and ask for a clear payment date.
8. If payment is still unpaid after 30 days, the owner writes to the client with a final due date, states any late fee that was in the terms, and says future bookings will need payment in full in advance.
**Quality check:** Before sending, check the invoice against the BEO line by line. The client should be able to see the quote total, the deposit, the extras, and the balance due, and reach the same total you did.
**If it goes wrong:** If you invoiced the wrong amount, correct it the same day, apologise, and send a new invoice with a new number. If the client disputes an extra charge, show them the BEO note or the hire record that supports it, and agree a fair outcome in writing.
**Records:** Keep the final invoice, the payment receipts, the reminders, and any notes about disputes or late fees in the client file until the balance is paid in full.
## SOP 10 — Handling dietary requirements and allergens
**Purpose:** To serve every guest safely, including guests with allergies, intolerances, and cultural or religious food rules, so no one is given the wrong dish by mistake.
**Scope:** This covers every dietary need told to Ember & Oak Catering before or during an event, from the first enquiry to the last plate served. It covers the kitchen, the floor team, and the bar.
**Who does it:** The bookings lead collects the dietary information. The head chef plans and prepares the special dishes, and the event manager makes sure the right dish reaches the right guest.
### Steps
1. Ask about dietary needs at the first enquiry, again when the quote is accepted, and again when final numbers are due. Ask for the guest's name, the exact need, and how serious it is, especially for allergies.
2. Write every dietary need on the BEO, with the guest's name and table number where you know it. Do not rely on memory, and never write "a few vegetarians" without a number.
3. Plan the special dishes with the chef at least seven days before the event. Decide what each guest will eat, how it will be cooked, and how it will be kept separate from other food.
4. Prepare allergen dishes first, on clean boards and with clean knives, pans, and tongs. Label them clearly in the kitchen, and keep them apart from other dishes on the pass.
5. Tell the floor team about every dietary guest in the briefing, by name and table. Give the server for that table a short list to carry, and make sure they know which dish goes to which guest.
6. Serve dietary dishes first, and say quietly to the guest what the dish is and that it was made for them. Check that the guest is happy before you walk away, and do not swap dishes between guests.
7. Keep the allergen information for every dish on hand during the event, including bought-in items such as bread, sauces, and desserts, so the team can answer honestly.
8. If a guest tells you about a dietary need on the day that was not on the BEO, tell the chef at once. Offer the safest dish you can make, explain what is in it, and record the new need.
**Quality check:** Before service, the chef and the event manager check the dietary list against the dishes on the pass, one guest at a time. Every special dish must have a name, a table, and a label.
**If it goes wrong:** If the wrong dish reaches a guest, take it away at once, apologise, and tell the chef. If a guest has an allergic reaction, call for medical help immediately, stay with the guest, and record exactly what they ate and when.
**Records:** Keep the dietary list, the guest names and table numbers, the dish plan, and any notes about changes on the day in the event file, with the BEO.
