# The Workshop SOP Pack
> Ten ready-to-use procedures for a small workshop. Written out in full — no blanks to fill in.

## SOP 01 — Opening the workshop
**Purpose:** To open Harbour Joinery safely and on time each morning, so the workshop is clean, well lit, and ready for work by 8:00 am, with no machine started before it has been checked.
**Scope:** This covers the whole workshop — the bench room, the machine room, the timber store, and the finishing room — every working day, from unlocking the door to the first job.
**Who does it:** The first person to arrive opens the workshop, usually the workshop lead. If the lead is away, the most experienced maker on duty takes over.
### Steps
1. Unlock the front door and the side gate. Switch off the alarm using the code on the card inside the front door. If the alarm sounds, enter the code again slowly.
2. Switch on the lights in the bench room, machine room, and timber store. Open the high windows in the machine room and switch on the dust extractor fan.
3. Walk through the workshop and look for anything out of place: water on the floor, a smell of gas or burning, a broken window, or tools left where someone could trip.
4. Check the whiteboard by the front door for today's jobs. Read the job list out loud with whoever is in, so everyone knows what they are working on.
5. Check the dust extractor bags and the bins under the saws. Empty any bag that is more than three-quarters full into the outside waste bin and put a fresh bag in.
6. Sweep the floor around each machine and clear the benches of offcuts and rubbish. Stack usable offcuts in the offcut rack by the timber store.
7. Turn on the compressor and let it build up pressure for five minutes. While it fills, check that the first-aid box by the sink is stocked and the fire extinguisher by the machine room door is in place.
8. Start the machines one at a time, following SOP 03, and let each one run for a minute while you listen for rattles or grinding. Stop any machine that sounds wrong and tag it out of use.
**Quality check:** Before anyone starts cutting, the opener walks the floor once more. Every machine runs smoothly, the floor is clear, the extractor is on, and the job board matches what is on the benches.
**If it goes wrong:** If the alarm will not switch off, leave the building and phone the alarm company number on the front door. If a machine sounds wrong, tag it with a red tag, write the problem on the tag, and tell the lead. Never run a machine you are worried about.
**Records:** Write the date, your name, and the time you opened in the logbook on the shelf by the front door. Note any machine tagged out of use and any delivery expected today.

## SOP 02 — Closing the workshop
**Purpose:** To close Harbour Joinery safely at the end of the day, so the workshop is locked, the machines are off, the dust is cleared, and tomorrow starts cleanly.
**Scope:** This covers the whole workshop at the end of every working day, including Saturdays — shutting down machines, cleaning up, locking up, and handing over anything unfinished.
**Who does it:** The last person to leave closes the workshop, usually the workshop lead. If the lead leaves early, they name one person to close before they go.
### Steps
1. At 4:30 pm, stop starting new cuts. Finish the cut or glue-up you are on, then clean your bench and put your tools back on the wall rack or in your toolbox.
2. Switch off each machine at the machine, then at the wall. Unplug the hand tools and coil their leads neatly. Put blades and cutters back in their cases — never leave a blade on a bench overnight.
3. Empty the dust extractor bags and the bins under the saws into the outside waste bin. Sweep the floors and brush the dust off the benches into a dustpan.
4. Check the glue-ups and finishes drying in the finishing room. Make sure each one is labelled with the job name and the date, and close the finishing room door to keep dust out.
5. Stack any timber leaning against walls flat on the timber rack. Put offcuts longer than 30 cm in the offcut rack and throw the rest in the waste bin.
6. Check the job board and update it: tick off finished jobs, move unfinished jobs to tomorrow, and write down anything a customer is waiting on.
7. Turn off the compressor and let the air out through the drain valve under the tank until it stops hissing. Switch off the lights in the finishing room, timber store, and machine room.
8. Lock the timber store and the finishing room. Check the back door and all windows are shut and locked, switch on the alarm, lock the front door, and test the handle.
**Quality check:** Before you lock the front door, stand in the bench room and look around. Every machine is off, every bench is clear, the floor is swept, the drying pieces are labelled, and the job board is up to date.
**If it goes wrong:** If a machine will not switch off, switch it off at the wall and tag it with a red tag. If you find a leak or a smell of gas, leave the building and phone the workshop lead. If the alarm will not set, check every door and window again, then phone the alarm company. Never leave the workshop unlocked overnight.
**Records:** Write the date, your name, and the time you closed in the logbook. Note any jobs carried over to tomorrow, any machine tagged out of use, and any messages left for the lead.

## SOP 03 — Setting up a machine safely
**Purpose:** To set up any workshop machine the same safe way every time, so the blade is right for the job, the guards are in place, and nobody gets hurt by a skipped check.
**Scope:** This covers every powered machine at Harbour Joinery — the table saw, band saw, planer-thicknesser, router table, drill press, and spindle moulder — before the first cut and whenever a blade or setup changes.
**Who does it:** Only a trained maker sets up a machine. A new helper may watch and help clean, but they do not change blades or adjust a machine until trained and signed off under SOP 10.
### Steps
1. Read the job sheet first. Check the material, its thickness, and the cut you need. Choose the right blade and check it is sharp and undamaged.
2. Switch the machine off at the wall before you touch anything. Unplug it if you are changing a blade or cutter. Never change a blade on a machine that is still plugged in.
3. Fit the blade, following the arrow showing which way it turns. Tighten the nut firmly with the spanner beside the machine, and turn the blade slowly by hand to check it runs true.
4. Set the fence and the depth of cut. Measure blade to fence with a tape measure, not by eye, and lock the fence. Set the blade about 5 mm above the timber.
5. Put the guard back on and check it moves freely and covers the blade. Check the riving knife is in line. If the guard is missing or broken, do not use the machine.
6. Check the dust extraction hose is connected and the extractor is on. Clear the table of tools, offcuts, and anything else that is not part of the cut.
7. Switch on and let it run for a minute, listening for rattles or grinding. Then make a test cut on a scrap piece and check it is clean and the right size.
8. Make the real cut, hands clear of the blade, using a push stick for cuts closer than 15 cm to it. Never reach over a spinning blade. When done, switch off, wait for the blade to stop, and brush the table clean.
**Quality check:** Before the first real cut, check three things: the guard is on, the fence measurement is right, and the test cut matches the job sheet. A second maker checks the spindle moulder setup.
**If it goes wrong:** If a blade jams, switch off at the wall and wait for it to stop before touching anything. If someone is hurt, switch off, give first aid, and phone for help. Report every accident to the lead the same day.
**Records:** Write the machine, blade fitted, and date in the machine logbook. Note blades needing sharpening and machines tagged out. The lead checks the logbook every Friday.

## SOP 04 — Receiving and checking materials
**Purpose:** To check every delivery of timber, boards, and hardware as it arrives, so damaged or wrong materials are spotted on the day they arrive — not halfway through a job when it is too late to send them back.
**Scope:** This covers all deliveries to Harbour Joinery: timber, sheet boards, veneer, hardware, glue, and finishes, from any supplier, at the front door or the side gate.
**Who does it:** The person on duty when the delivery arrives receives it, usually the workshop lead or the most experienced maker in the workshop. A new helper may carry materials inside, but they do not sign for a delivery.
### Steps
1. When the driver arrives, ask them to wait while you check the delivery. Do not sign anything until you have looked at the goods — a signature means you have accepted them.
2. Count pieces against the delivery note. If short, write the real count on the note before signing.
3. Check each piece for cracks, splits, warping, knots right through, and damp patches. Sight along each board — if it bends more than a few millimetres, set it aside.
4. Measure a few boards with a tape measure to check they are the size ordered. Check sheet boards for chipped corners and swollen edges, and check hardware packs are sealed and the right size.
5. If anything is damaged or wrong, photograph it, write the problem on the note, and tell the driver. Phone or email the supplier the same day.
6. Carry materials inside straight away. Stack timber flat, with thin strips between layers so air moves around it. Stand sheet boards upright in the rack.
7. Store finishes, glue, and aerosols in the metal cabinet in the finishing room, lids on tight and away from heat. Check the date on each tin — use the oldest first.
8. Put small hardware — screws, hinges, handles — in the labelled bins above the bench, sorted by size and type. Label job materials with the job name, and tell the lead what arrived and flag anything damaged or short.
**Quality check:** Before the materials go on the rack, check three things: the count matches the note, the sizes match the order, and nothing is damaged or warped. If any one fails, note the problem and contact the supplier the same day.
**If it goes wrong:** If a delivery arrives damaged after the driver has left, take photos and phone the supplier within the hour. If the wrong timber arrives, do not cut it — keep it aside for collection. If materials arrive wet, stack them with strips between the layers and let them dry for a week.
**Records:** File the signed note in the folder by the front door, under the supplier's name. Log the delivery: date, supplier, what arrived, and any problems. The lead checks the logbook monthly against invoices.

## SOP 05 — Taking a customer order
**Purpose:** To take a customer order clearly and completely the first time, so the workshop knows exactly what to make, what it should look like, and when it is wanted.
**Scope:** This covers every order at Harbour Joinery, whether the customer walks in, phones, or emails — custom furniture, repairs, and repeat orders, from the first enquiry to the job sheet going on the board.
**Who does it:** The workshop lead takes orders, or the most experienced maker if the lead is away. A new helper may take a message, but they do not agree prices or dates with a customer.
### Steps
1. Greet the customer by name if you know it, and listen first. Let them describe what they want before you ask questions.
2. Ask the key questions and write answers as you go: What is it? What size? What timber or finish? Where will it go? When is it needed?
3. Ask about details that cause trouble later: a particular colour? Something it must match? A doorway or staircase it must fit through? Collect or deliver?
4. If the customer has a photo or sketch, ask them to email it or leave a copy. Write the date received on the back and keep it with the order.
5. Take the customer's full name, phone number, and email, and read the number back to check it. Ask how they prefer to be contacted.
6. Be honest about timing. Check the job board before promising a date. If busy, say so and offer the earliest realistic date.
7. Explain the next step: a quote within two working days, and work starts only once they accept it and pay the deposit. Do not promise a price on the spot.
8. Write everything on a job sheet: customer details, what they want, sizes, timber, finish, date wanted, and the job number. Read it back to the customer before they leave, so mistakes are caught now.
**Quality check:** Before the job sheet goes in the folder, check it has five things: customer name and phone number, what to make, the sizes, the date wanted, and the job number. If any is missing, fill it in now.
**If it goes wrong:** If a customer changes the order, write the change on the job sheet with the date and tell the lead so the quote can be updated. If you promised a date the workshop cannot meet, phone the customer as soon as you know and offer a new date. If a customer is unhappy, listen, do not argue, and pass it to the lead.
**Records:** File the job sheet in the orders folder, with photos or sketches attached. Write the job number, customer name, and date in the job book. When the quote is sent, write the date on the sheet. The lead checks the "Quotes to do" list every morning.

## SOP 06 — Quoting a job
**Purpose:** To price every job fairly and consistently, so the customer gets a clear written quote, the workshop covers its costs, and nobody guesses a price on the spot.
**Scope:** This covers every quote — custom furniture, repairs, and repeat work — sent by email or in person, and every change made after it has been sent.
**Who does it:** The workshop lead writes the quote, or the most experienced maker if the lead is away. A new helper does not quote jobs. If a job is large or unusual, the lead talks it through with the workshop first.
### Steps
1. Read the job sheet and any photos. If anything is unclear, phone the customer before pricing.
2. Work out materials first. List every item the job needs, with size and amount. Price each from the supplier's list or the last invoice, and add 10 percent for waste.
3. Work out the time. Break the job into steps — cutting, jointing, assembly, sanding, finishing — and estimate hours for each. Be honest: finishing always takes a full day.
4. Work out labour: hours times the hourly rate on the rate card above the bench. Add delivery, from distance and piece size.
5. Add materials, labour, and delivery, then add the workshop markup from the rate card. Round the total to the nearest pound or dollar.
6. Write the quote on the quote sheet or in an email. Include the job number, customer name, a plain description of the work, timber and finish, price, deposit, and the expiry date — 30 days from when you send it.
7. State the payment terms: a 50 percent deposit before work starts, and the balance on completion before delivery or collection. State what is not included, such as fitting the piece at the customer's home.
8. Read the quote through before sending. Check the name is spelled right, the price adds up, and the date matches the job sheet. Send it by email, file a copy with the job sheet, and tell the customer it is on its way.
**Quality check:** Before a quote goes out, check four things: materials list complete, hours realistic, price adds up, and deposit and payment terms written down. Fix anything wrong before the customer sees it.
**If it goes wrong:** If you underpriced a job after the customer accepted, tell the lead straight away — do not cut corners to make up the difference. If the customer asks for a discount, do not agree on the spot; say you will check and phone back. If they want changes after accepting, write a new quote for the changed work first.
**Records:** Keep a copy of every quote with the job sheet, and write the job number, customer name, amount, and date sent in the job book. When a deposit is paid, write the amount and date on the sheet. The lead checks the job book every Friday.

## SOP 07 — Quality check before dispatch
**Purpose:** To check every finished piece carefully before it leaves Harbour Joinery, so the customer receives work the workshop is proud of — no missed sanding, no wobbly legs, no damage picked up at the last minute.
**Scope:** This covers every finished piece before it is packed or collected: custom furniture, repairs, and repeat orders. The check happens in the bench room, in good light, before the piece is wrapped.
**Who does it:** A maker who did not build the piece checks it, so it is seen with fresh eyes. The lead signs off large or expensive pieces. The maker fixes anything the checker finds.
### Steps
1. Clear a bench or floor space and put the piece where you can walk around it. Switch on the bench light — you cannot check a finish properly in a dim corner.
2. Check sizes first. Measure length, width, and height and compare them to the job sheet. Check the piece fits through a standard doorway.
3. Check the piece is flat and level. Put it on the bench and press each corner — it should not rock. Check doors and drawers open and close smoothly, and level the top.
4. Run your hand over every surface, including underside and back. Feel for rough patches, splinters, glue spots, and missed sanding. Mark rough spots lightly in pencil.
5. Look at the finish from different angles. Check for drips, brush marks, bare patches, and dust in the finish. Check the colour matches the agreed sample.
6. Check every joint for gaps, open seams, or glue squeezing out. Check screws are tight and flush, and handles, hinges, and knobs are straight and secure.
7. Check the piece against the job sheet once more: right timber, right finish, right handles, and any special detail asked for.
8. If you find a problem, write it on the job sheet and hand the piece back to its maker. When it passes, checker and maker both sign with the date, and the piece moves to the dispatch area by the front door.
**Quality check:** A piece only passes four tests: right size, flat and stable, smooth surfaces with an even finish, and sound joints and fittings. If it fails any one, it goes back to the maker.
**If it goes wrong:** If the finish is damaged or uneven, the piece goes back to the finishing room — do not hide a bad finish with wax or polish. If a joint has opened, the maker re-glues and clamps it and it is checked again the next day. If a piece fails near the delivery date, phone the customer, explain honestly, and agree a new date.
**Records:** Keep the signed job sheet in the orders folder, with the checker's name and check date on it. Write faults found and how they were fixed on the back. The lead reads the notes monthly to spot recurring problems.

## SOP 08 — Packing and shipping an order
**Purpose:** To pack and ship every order so it arrives in the same condition it left the workshop — protected, labelled, and tracked — and the customer knows it is on its way.
**Scope:** This covers all orders leaving Harbour Joinery: pieces collected by the customer, pieces delivered by the workshop van, and pieces sent by courier. It covers packing, labelling, and telling the customer the order is coming.
**Who does it:** The maker who built the piece packs it, with help for large or heavy pieces. The lead books the courier or the van. A new helper may help wrap, but does not pack a piece alone.
### Steps
1. Confirm on the job sheet that the piece passed its quality check under SOP 07. Do not pack a piece that has not been signed off.
2. Lay out packing materials on a clean bench: bubble wrap, foam sheets, corner protectors, tape, and a marker. Hands clean, bench dust-free, so nothing scratches the finish.
3. Wrap the piece in a soft cloth or foam sheet first, so bubble wrap cannot scratch the finish. Then wrap in bubble wrap, bubbles out, and tape firmly.
4. Fit corner protectors on every corner and edge, and tape them in place. For a table or cabinet, wrap each leg separately and put foam between parts that could rub.
5. If the piece goes in a box, choose one with room for padding on all sides. Fill gaps with scrunched paper or foam so it cannot move. Seal it and write "FRAGILE — THIS WAY UP" on top.
6. Write the customer's name, address, and phone number clearly on the label and stick it outside where it can be read. Put a second copy inside the box, in case the outside one comes off.
7. If it goes by courier, book the collection and write the tracking number on the job sheet. If it goes in the van, load it with another person, strap it in, and put a blanket underneath.
8. Photograph the packed piece before it leaves, then phone or email the customer to say it is on its way, with the tracking number or delivery time.
**Quality check:** Before a parcel leaves, check three things: every corner wrapped and protected, label name and address right, and customer told it is coming. Lift gently — if anything moves or rattles, open it and pack again.
**If it goes wrong:** If a piece is damaged while packing, stop, tell the lead, and follow SOP 09 — do not pack a damaged piece and hope the customer will not notice. If the courier does not collect, phone them the same day and rebook. If the label is wrong, write a new one.
**Records:** Keep the job sheet in the finished jobs folder, with tracking number, delivery date, and customer name on it. Keep the courier receipt with it. The lead checks the folder every Friday.

## SOP 09 — Handling a mistake or damaged work
**Purpose:** To deal with a mistake or damaged piece quickly, honestly, and calmly, so the customer is looked after and the same mistake does not happen twice.
**Scope:** This covers any mistake: a piece cut to the wrong size, a damaged finish, a broken delivery, an unhappy customer, or damage found during the quality check.
**Who does it:** Whoever spots the problem reports it to the lead straight away — whoever made it, whoever found it. The lead decides how to put it right and who talks to the customer. Nobody hides a mistake.
### Steps
1. Stop work as soon as you spot the problem. Do not carry on and hope it will not matter — it will cost more to fix later.
2. Tell the lead the same day, and be straight about what went wrong, when, and why. No blame for an honest mistake reported quickly.
3. Photograph the damage and write what happened on the job sheet: date, what went wrong, and how big the problem is.
4. Work out with the lead: repair or remake? A scratched finish can often be sanded and refinished; a board cut 20 mm too short usually cannot be saved.
5. If it can be repaired, agree who does it and by when. If it must be remade, find matching timber in the store — same batch if possible.
6. If the delivery date is affected, phone the customer as soon as you know. Explain in plain words, say sorry, and give a new realistic date. Most accept a delay if told early and honestly.
7. If the customer is unhappy, listen fully before answering. Do not argue or blame them. Write down what is wrong and pass it to the lead, who phones back the same day.
8. Agree the fix with the customer first: repair, remake, or — if neither is possible — refund or discount. Write the agreement on the job sheet. Then do the work to the same standard and put the piece through the full check under SOP 07.
**Quality check:** A repaired or remade piece only leaves when it passes the same quality check as any other piece, and the lead has seen it. If the repair is visible — a patch, a colour difference, a different-looking joint — it is not good enough, and the piece is remade.
**If it goes wrong:** If a customer is angry, stay calm, listen, and promise nothing you cannot deliver — say the lead will phone back today. If the same mistake happens three times, the lead stops the job and works out what must change — procedure, tool, or training.
**Records:** Write every mistake on the job sheet and in the logbook by the front door: date, job number, what happened, what caused it, and how it was fixed. The lead reads it monthly to spot patterns and change the procedure so the problem stops.

## SOP 10 — Training a new helper
**Purpose:** To train a new helper properly in their first two weeks, so they know how the workshop works, can do simple jobs safely, and feel welcome.
**Scope:** This covers a new helper's first two weeks — safety, the basic jobs, and how progress is checked.
**Who does it:** The lead is responsible for training, and names one experienced maker as the helper's buddy for two weeks. The buddy shows the work, watches them do it, and answers questions. The helper does not work alone until signed off.
### Steps
1. On the first morning, the lead welcomes the helper and shows them around — toilets, kitchen corner, first-aid box, fire exits, and the extinguisher by the machine room door.
2. Go through the safety rules: glasses and ear protection in the machine room, closed shoes with a hard toe, hair tied back, no loose clothing or gloves near a blade. Give them their own glasses and ear protectors.
3. Show the logbook, job board, and job sheets, and explain how a job moves from order to finished piece. Walk them through SOP 01 and SOP 02.
4. On day one, give simple, safe jobs: sweeping, stacking offcuts, carrying timber, sanding, and cleaning benches. Stay nearby and check their work kindly.
5. Teach one machine at a time, starting with the safest — drill press or sander. Show how it works, let them watch, then let them try it while you stand beside them.
6. By the end of week one, they should open the workshop with you, set up a simple job, and sand and finish a piece to standard. Record each skill on the sheet and sign it as learned.
7. In week two, give them a small real job — a chopping board, a shelf, or a box — and let them follow it from job sheet to quality check. The buddy checks each stage.
8. At each day's end, spend five minutes together: what went well, what was tricky, what to learn tomorrow. At the end of week two, lead, buddy, and helper go through the sheet together.
**Quality check:** A helper is signed off only when they can do a task safely, unwatched, and to standard — and the buddy has seen them do it twice. If they are not ready, they get more practice, not more pressure.
**If it goes wrong:** If a helper makes a mistake, treat it as a training point, not a telling-off — show them what went wrong and how to do it right. If they feel unsafe on a machine, stop and let them step back. If they are struggling after two weeks, the lead talks honestly and agrees a plan with them.
**Records:** Keep the training sheet in the staff folder: helper name, start date, buddy name, each task learned, sign-off date, and the lead's signature. The lead reviews every training sheet to make sure the training works.
