## SOP 01 — Customer enquiry and site visit
**Purpose:** To handle every new enquiry the same way, so no job is promised before Northgate Welding & Fabrication knows what it involves, and no customer is left waiting for an answer.
**Scope:** Covers all new enquiries by phone, email, text or walk-in, for gates, railings, stairs, repairs and custom steelwork, from first contact to the site visit notes. Pricing is SOP 02.
**Who does it:** The owner takes enquiries and does site visits. If the owner is out, the lead welder takes the details and passes them on the same day.
### Steps
1. Answer the same day, or first thing the next working morning. Take the customer's name, phone, address, what they want, its rough size, where it goes, and when they need it.
2. Ask what the steelwork is for. A driveway gate, a balcony railing and a trailer frame repair are different jobs, with different fixings and safety rules.
3. Ask about access. Can a truck and trailer get in? Note steps, slopes, narrow gates or parked cars that will make delivery hard.
4. Ask for drawings or photos, or an old gate to copy. Ask the customer to send photos by text or email, and save them with the enquiry.
5. Book a site visit within three working days for any job over $500, and for any job where measurements matter. For a small repair, a photo and a call may be enough.
6. At the visit, measure twice. Measure the opening top, middle and bottom, because walls and posts are rarely square. Write down the smallest measurement.
7. Check what the new work fixes to. Push on posts and walls, look for cracks, rot or rust, and note if a post needs replacing first.
8. Ask about power, water, gas lines and buried cables where posts will be dug or drilled, and mark them on the sketch.
9. Photograph the site and fixing points. Sketch the job with measurements, and note the customer's choices: style, colour, infill, hinges, latch and lock.
10. Before leaving, agree what happens next. Say when the written quote will arrive, usually within three working days, and leave a card with the shop number.
**Quality check:** Before leaving, read the measurements back to the customer. Back at the shop, check the form is complete before it goes to estimating.
**If it goes wrong:** If a measurement is missed or a fixing point is rotten or loose, call the customer at once, explain what changed, and book a second visit before quoting. Never guess a measurement.
**Records:** Keep the enquiry form, sketch, site photos and customer photos in the job folder, filed under the customer's name and date.
## SOP 02 — Estimating and quoting a job
**Purpose:** To price every job the same way, so Northgate Welding & Fabrication covers its steel, consumables, labour and travel, and the customer gets a clear written quote they can trust.
**Scope:** Covers all quotes for gates, railings, stairs, repairs and custom steelwork, from the site visit notes to the written quote. Taking the deposit is SOP 03.
**Who does it:** The owner prices every job. The lead welder checks the labour time and cutting list before the quote goes out. Nobody else sends a price to a customer.
### Steps
1. Start from the site visit notes and sketch. If a measurement is missing or unclear, go back to the customer or the site. Never price from a guess.
2. Work out the steel list: every length of tube, flat bar, angle, plate and mesh, with sizes. Add 10 percent for cutting waste, and price at the latest supplier price.
3. Add consumables: welding wire or rods, gas, cutting and grinding discs, drill bits, bolts, hinges, latches, locks, paint and primer. List them — small items add up.
4. Work out labour time honestly. Allow time for cutting, welding, grinding, painting, fitting, loading and travel. If a job took six hours before, do not quote four now.
5. Add outside costs: galvanising, powder coating, a hire truck, or a second person for a heavy lift. Get a real price for these before quoting.
6. Add travel and site time for fitting, including a return trip if the job needs two visits — one to fit posts, one to hang the gate, for example.
7. Add the shop margin on top of costs. Check the total against two or three similar past jobs. If it is far higher or lower, find out why first.
8. Write the quote on shop letterhead: date, customer, a plain description of the work, steel and finish included, total price, tax, price valid 30 days, and deposit needed.
9. State what is not included: building permits, concrete work by others, electrical work, removing an old gate unless agreed, and repainting after customer damage.
10. Send the quote by email or hand it over in person, and note the date sent. Follow up once by phone after five working days. Do not chase a customer more than twice.
**Quality check:** Before sending, the lead welder reads the quote and steel list together and checks sizes, quantities and labour time make sense. The owner signs every quote.
**If it goes wrong:** If steel prices jump or a mistake is found after the quote is sent, tell the customer before work starts and issue a corrected quote in writing. If they already accepted, honour the price unless they agree to a change in writing.
**Records:** Keep a copy of every quote, with the steel list and labour workings, in the job folder. Note the date sent, the follow-up call, and whether the customer accepted or declined.
## SOP 03 — Order acceptance and deposits
**Purpose:** To turn an accepted quote into a firm order with a deposit, a start date and a written record, so the shop never starts cutting steel for a job that is not confirmed.
**Scope:** Covers accepting a quote, taking a deposit, agreeing dates and ordering materials. It applies to every job over $200. Small cash repairs under $200 are paid on completion.
**Who does it:** The owner accepts orders and takes deposits. The lead welder confirms the start date fits the workshop diary. The owner orders the steel.
### Steps
1. When a customer says yes, ask them to confirm in writing — a signed quote, an email or a text saying "go ahead" with the quote number. A verbal yes is not enough.
2. Confirm the details once more before taking money: design, measurements, colour, finish, hinges, latch and lock, and where the work will be fitted. Reprice anything that changed.
3. Take a deposit of 50 percent before ordering steel, by bank transfer, card or cash. Give a receipt every time, showing the job number, amount paid and balance left.
4. Give the customer a realistic start date and finish date, and write both on the order form. Allow extra days for galvanising or powder coating if needed.
5. Open a job folder and job card. Write the job number, customer name, address, phone, work description, price, deposit paid, balance due, start date and finish date. Pin the card on the job board.
6. Order the steel and fixings the same day the deposit clears. Check the delivery date before promising anything. If steel is short or delayed, tell the customer the same day.
7. Book any outside work — galvanising, powder coating or a hire truck — as soon as the steel is ordered, so the slot is held.
8. If the customer wants changes after accepting, stop, write the change down with any new price, and get agreement in writing before extra work is done. Changes are SOP 10.
9. The day before work starts, check the job card, steel delivery and diary all line up. If anything is missing, call the customer before the start date, not on the morning fitters are due.
10. File the signed quote, deposit receipt and order form in the job folder before any cutting starts.
**Quality check:** Before ordering steel, the owner checks the job card against the signed quote — same price, same design, same finish. If anything does not match, stop and sort it out first.
**If it goes wrong:** If a deposit does not clear, or the customer changes their mind before steel is ordered, refund it in full and close the job card. If steel is already cut, the deposit covers steel and labour done so far.
**Records:** Keep the signed quote, deposit receipt, order form and job card in the job folder. Note the deposit amount, date paid, payment method and balance due.
## SOP 04 — Pre-work safety and hot work check
**Purpose:** To make sure nobody gets hurt and nothing catches fire before any cutting, welding or grinding starts, in the workshop or on site.
**Scope:** Covers every working day and job site: protective equipment, the workshop check, gas bottles, fume extraction and the hot work check. It applies to all three staff and anyone helping.
**Who does it:** The lead welder does the daily workshop check. Each welder checks their own protective equipment and work area before starting. The owner checks site safety on the first visit.
### Steps
1. Put on protective equipment before touching tools: welding helmet with the correct shade, safety glasses for grinding, leather gloves, fire-resistant clothing, steel-toe boots and hearing protection.
2. Check the helmet lens is clean and uncracked, gloves have no holes, and the jacket has no frayed cuffs. Replace damaged gear before starting.
3. Clear the work area. Sweep up dust, paper and offcuts, and move fuel, paint and solvents at least 10 metres from welding, or into the fireproof cabinet.
4. Check the fire extinguisher is in place, in date and within reach, and the fire blanket is on its hook. Keep water or sand nearby on site.
5. Check gas bottles are chained upright, caps on when not in use, and stored away from heat. Check hoses and gauges for cracks, leaks or damage. Never use oil or grease on a gas fitting.
6. Switch fume extraction on before welding, arm within about 30 cm of the weld, doors open for airflow. Wear a respirator for galvanised steel or tight spaces.
7. Check welders, grinders and drills before use for damaged cables, cracked plugs and loose connections. Tag out any damaged tool until repaired.
8. On site, do a hot work check before striking an arc. Move timber, dry leaves, insulation, fuel and paint within 10 metres away, or cover them with a fire blanket, and agree where welding will happen.
9. Keep a fire watch after hot work. Check the work area 30 minutes after the last weld or cut for smoke or glowing embers, especially in wall cavities and under floors.
10. At day's end, turn off the welder and the gas at the bottle, coil the leads, and sweep up. Report damaged equipment, a near miss or an injury to the owner the same day.
**Quality check:** The lead welder signs the daily safety checklist each morning. No welding starts until it is signed. On site, the welder doing the job runs the hot work check.
**If it goes wrong:** If a fire starts, stop work, raise the alarm, use the extinguisher only if safe and the fire is small, and call the fire service. If someone is injured, give first aid and call for help, and do not move them.
**Records:** Keep the signed daily checklist, hot work notes for site jobs, and a record of every incident, near miss and damaged tool in the safety file.
## SOP 05 — Cutting and fit-up
**Purpose:** To cut steel accurately and fit parts together square and true, so welding is quick, the finished work fits the opening, and nothing has to be forced into place on site.
**Scope:** Covers measuring, marking, cutting and fitting up all steelwork in the workshop — gates, railings, stairs, frames and repairs. Welding is SOP 06.
**Who does it:** The lead welder and the second welder cut and fit up work. The owner checks fit-up on one-off or complex jobs before welding starts.
### Steps
1. Read the job card and sketch before cutting anything. Check measurements, steel sizes and finish, and ask if anything is unclear. Do not cut from memory.
2. Check steel delivered against the order. Set aside bent lengths, heavy rust or the wrong size. Measure each length before cutting.
3. Mark out clearly with soapstone or marker, measuring twice. Mark the cut line all the way round the tube or bar, not just on one face, so the cut stays square.
4. Cut with the right tool: chop saw for straight cuts on tube and bar, plasma cutter or oxy-fuel torch for plate, and bandsaw for thin sections.
5. Keep hands clear of the blade and the hot cut end. Wear gloves for cut steel, safety glasses at all times, and a face shield with the plasma cutter or grinder. Clamp the work — never hold it by hand.
6. Clean up every cut end. Grind off burrs and sharp edges, and clean mill scale, rust and paint from the joint area, so the weld bites into clean metal.
7. Lay parts out on the welding table and fit them up dry, without welding. Check the frame is square by measuring corner to corner — both diagonals should match.
8. Clamp the frame, tack it in a few places, then measure again. Check it is flat and not twisted by laying a straight edge across it. Fix any twist now, before full welding.
9. Check hinges, latches, locks and fixing plates fit where they belong. Offer the hardware up to the frame and mark bolt holes before welding.
10. Write the job number on the frame with a marker, and store fitted-up work flat or upright where it cannot be knocked over or walked on.
**Quality check:** Before welding, measure the fitted frame once more — length, width and both diagonals — and compare it with the job card. If it is more than 3 mm out, adjust it now.
**If it goes wrong:** If a part is cut short or the frame is out of square, stop and re-cut the part. Do not stretch, bend or force a frame to fit, and do not weld over a bad fit-up. Tell the owner if a re-cut will delay the job.
**Records:** Keep the cutting list with lengths used, noted on the job card. Note any steel wasted or re-cut and why, so the next estimate is right.
## SOP 06 — Welding and inspection
**Purpose:** To weld every joint cleanly and strongly, and to check the work as it is made, so gates, railings and frames hold up in daily use and pass inspection first time.
**Scope:** Covers all MIG welding of mild steel in the workshop and on site, repairs, and the visual inspection of every weld. Cutting and fit-up is SOP 05; grinding and painting is SOP 07.
**Who does it:** The lead welder and the second welder do the welding. The lead welder inspects every finished piece. The owner spot-checks one job a week.
### Steps
1. Check fit-up before welding — square, flat, tacked and measured, as in SOP 05. Do not weld a frame that has not been checked.
2. Set the welder for the steel thickness. Test-weld scrap of the same thickness and adjust wire speed and voltage until the weld sounds steady and looks smooth.
3. Clean the joint again if it has picked up rust, paint or oil since fit-up. A dirty joint makes a weak weld, with holes and slag trapped inside.
4. Weld in a planned order, not all on one side. Work opposite sides in turn and let the metal cool between runs, so the frame does not pull or twist.
5. Keep a steady travel speed and a short, even arc. Watch the weld pool, not the arc light. Stop and clean the weld if it starts to spit or pile up.
6. For railings and balconies, weld all round each joint where the design calls for it. Do not leave the back of a joint unwelded because nobody will see it.
7. Chip off slag and brush each weld as you go, so you can see what you have done before the next run. Do not weld over slag, rust or a cold lap.
8. Let the work cool naturally. Do not quench a hot weld in water to hurry it, and do not move a frame while the welds are still hot and soft.
9. Inspect every weld by eye and hand once cool. Look for cracks, holes, undercut, lack of fusion, and welds too small or too big. Feel for sharp edges or gaps.
10. Mark any weld that needs repair with chalk, grind it out fully, and re-weld it. Do not paint over a bad weld or fill it with filler to hide it.
**Quality check:** The lead welder checks the finished piece against the job card — size, shape, hinge and latch positions, and every weld inspected. No job moves to finishing until the card is initialled.
**If it goes wrong:** If a frame twists, clamp it flat and use heat carefully to straighten it, or cut the joint, re-fit and re-weld. If a weld keeps failing, check settings, gas flow and steel cleanliness, then test-weld scrap.
**Records:** Note the welder, date welded and any re-welds on the job card. Keep a note of recurring weld faults and what fixed them.
## SOP 07 — Finishing — grinding and painting
**Purpose:** To finish every piece of steelwork smooth, clean and well protected, so it looks good on the day it is fitted and does not rust in the first year.
**Scope:** Covers grinding, cleaning, priming and painting all workshop steelwork, and work that goes for galvanising or powder coating. Welding is SOP 06; fitting is SOP 08.
**Who does it:** The second welder usually does grinding and painting, checked by the lead welder. The owner agrees the finish with the customer at the quoting stage.
### Steps
1. Let the work cool fully before grinding. Check welds are sound first — grinding cannot fix a bad weld or hide one.
2. Grind welds smooth where seen or touched — handrails, gate tops, edges. On hidden joints, knock off sharp edges and spatter.
3. Wear safety glasses, a face shield, gloves, hearing protection and a dust mask when grinding. Keep the guard on, and check the disc for cracks before switching on.
4. Clean the whole piece before painting. Wire-brush off rust and loose scale, wipe off dust and oil, and make sure the steel is dry. Paint will not stick to oily or damp steel.
5. Treat outside bare steel with rust-inhibiting primer. Cover joints, corners and tube ends evenly, and let it dry for the time on the tin before the top coat.
6. Apply the top coat in thin, even coats, not one thick coat that runs and sags. Two thin coats beat one thick one. Let the first dry fully before the second.
7. Seal open tube ends where water could get in, or drill a small drain hole at the lowest point of an outside frame, so rainwater cannot sit inside and rust it.
8. For galvanising or powder coating, grind sharp edges smooth, drill vent holes in sealed tubes, and remove paint, oil and labels. Send it with job number and finish on the delivery note.
9. When work comes back from the coater, check it first for bare patches, rough lumps, missed corners and transit damage, and touch up small marks with matching paint.
10. Store finished work under cover, off the floor, and do not stack painted pieces face to face without padding. Tag each piece with the job number and customer name.
**Quality check:** The lead welder looks over the finished piece in good light before it leaves — smooth where it should be smooth, even paint, no runs, no bare patches, no sharp edges. If it is not good enough, it goes back for another coat.
**If it goes wrong:** If paint bubbles, peels or runs, strip the bad area to bare metal, clean it and repaint. If a galvanised finish comes back damaged, send it back or touch it up with cold galvanising paint.
**Records:** Note the finish used — primer, paint type and colour, or galvanising or powder coating — on the job card, with date finished and who did it.
## SOP 08 — Dispatch and installation
**Purpose:** To get finished steelwork to the customer's site in one piece, and to fit it square, level and secure, so it works properly from the first day.
**Scope:** Covers loading, transport and fitting on site for gates, railings, stairs and custom steelwork, and the tools the team takes. Handover is SOP 09.
**Who does it:** Two people fit every job — the lead welder plus the second welder or the owner. Nobody fits a gate or railing alone: the pieces are heavy, and a second pair of hands keeps the work square.
### Steps
1. Before loading, check the finished piece against the job card — right job, size and colour, and the hardware bag with hinges, latch and touch-up paint.
2. Protect the work for transport. Wrap painted edges, pad pieces where they touch, and strap the load down. Check trailer lights before leaving.
3. Load drill, bits, level, tape, spanners, grinder, welder if needed, extension lead, fire extinguisher, fire blanket, PPE and the job folder.
4. On arrival, walk the site with the customer. Agree where the work goes, the truck parks and tools and offcuts go. Check the opening and fixing points again.
5. Set out the work before fixing anything. Offer the gate or railing up, check it is level and plumb, and mark the fixing points. Pack and shim where walls or posts are not straight.
6. Fix posts first where new posts are needed. Dig to the sketch depth, set the post plumb, brace it, pour concrete, and let it set before hanging weight. Never hang a gate on a wet post.
7. Bolt or weld the work in place as the job card says. Use the right fixings — sleeve anchors for concrete, coach screws for timber, rawl bolts for brick — and tighten fully.
8. Hang gates and check them: free swing, no dragging, clean latch. Adjust the hinges now, while the tools are out, not after sign-off.
9. Clean up as you go. Remove offcuts, sweep up dust and swarf, and take away the old gate or railing only if agreed on the quote. Leave the site as tidy as you found it.
10. Do a final walk-round with the customer before packing up. Show them the work, point out paint or concrete still to set, and tell them not to slam a new gate for 24 hours.
**Quality check:** Before leaving, check fixings are tight, gates swing and latch, railings are firm, and the work is level and plumb. The lead welder signs the installation check.
**If it goes wrong:** If a fixing point crumbles, a post moves, or the opening differs from the visit, stop, tell the customer what happened and what it will take to fix, and agree extra work and cost in writing first.
**Records:** Note the fitting date, who fitted it, site changes and extra work agreed on the job card. Keep the signed installation check in the job folder.
## SOP 09 — Handover and sign-off
**Purpose:** To hand finished work over to the customer properly, show them how it works, get their sign-off in writing, and collect the final payment without awkwardness or argument.
**Scope:** Covers the handover walk-round, demonstration, sign-off form, final invoice and payment, and care advice. It applies to every fitted job and workshop collection.
**Who does it:** The lead welder does the handover walk-round on site. The owner sends the final invoice and collects payment. Nobody leaves a job without sign-off or an agreed reason why.
### Steps
1. Finish the installation check first (SOP 08). Do not start handover until the work is complete, the site is tidy and tools are packed away.
2. Walk the customer round the finished work. Show them the gate, railing or frame, how it opens and closes, where the latch and lock are, and anything new.
3. Show the customer how to use it: the latch, locking and unlocking the gate, lifting a drop bolt, and what to do if a gate sticks in wet weather. Let them try it.
4. Point out the finish and how to look after it: wash paintwork with soapy water, touch up chips promptly with the paint left behind, and keep hinges oiled.
5. Say plainly what is guaranteed and for how long — workmanship for 12 months, paint or coating as the supplier states — and what is not covered, such as vehicle damage or misuse.
6. Ask the customer to look carefully and raise anything they are not happy with now, while fitters are still on site. Small adjustments are free at handover; a later return visit is not.
7. Fill in the sign-off form with the customer: job number, date, a short description of the work, and a line saying the work is complete and accepted. Both sign it.
8. Leave a care sheet with the finish, paint colour, care advice and the shop number, plus touch-up paint where agreed.
9. Send the final invoice the same day, showing quoted price, deposit paid and balance due, with payment due in 7 days. Send it by email and note the date.
10. When the balance is paid, send a receipt and mark the job card paid and complete. Move the card off the live board and file the job folder.
**Quality check:** Before leaving, check the form is signed and the customer has tried the gate or latch. If they will not sign, write down why and tell the owner the same day.
**If it goes wrong:** If the customer is unhappy, listen, write down exactly what is wrong, and do not argue on site. Tell them the owner will call within one working day, and pass the note on. If payment is late, the owner calls once, then sends a reminder after 14 days.
**Records:** Keep the signed sign-off form, care sheet, final invoice and payment receipt in the job folder. Note the handover date and who signed.
## SOP 10 — Changes, defects and rework
**Purpose:** To handle changes, complaints and faults calmly and in writing, so the customer knows what will happen, the shop knows who pays, and the same fault does not happen again.
**Scope:** Covers any change after a quote is accepted, any defect found by the shop or the customer, and any rework or repair under guarantee, in the workshop or after fitting.
**Who does it:** The owner decides price changes and guarantee claims. The lead welder decides how a defect is fixed. The owner talks about money; the welder talks about the fix.
### Steps
1. When a customer asks for a change, write it down exactly, with the date. Do not start until price and finish date effect are agreed in writing.
2. Price the change fairly: extra steel, labour and outside costs, less what is no longer needed. Give the new price in writing and get it signed first. Say in writing if small changes are free.
3. If the shop finds a defect before delivery — a failed weld, an unsquare frame, peeled paint — stop the job, mark the fault, and fix it before the job moves on.
4. If a customer reports a defect after fitting, thank them, write down the problem, and book a visit within three working days. Ask for a photo first.
5. At the visit, find the cause, not just the symptom. Check weld, fixings, posts and ground. A dropping gate may mean a loose hinge, a moving post or an unsquare frame.
6. Decide who pays. If the fault is the shop's workmanship or materials, fix it free under the 12-month guarantee. If the customer or a third party damaged it, quote it as a new job, price agreed first.
7. Fix the defect properly, not with a quick patch. Grind out a bad weld and re-weld it, replace a bent hinge, reset a post in concrete — do it as new work, and inspect it the same way.
8. After the fix, show the customer what was done and why, and get them to sign the rework note. Leave the area tidy.
9. Write up what went wrong and why, and discuss it at the next shop meeting. If the same fault happened twice, change how the work is done and note it.
**Quality check:** After rework, the lead welder inspects the repair and initials the rework note. A repaired job is checked like a new job, and signed off only when it passes.
**If it goes wrong:** If a customer is not happy after a repair, or the same fault comes back a third time, the owner takes over personally, visits the site, and agrees a way forward in writing: a further repair, a replacement part or a partial refund.
**Records:** Keep the change agreement, defect note, rework note and photos in the job folder. Keep a simple log of defects and changes, with cause and fix, so patterns show.
