## SOP 01 — Answering a new customer enquiry

**Purpose:** To answer every new enquiry quickly, politely and completely, so a potential customer gets a helpful reply the same working day and knows exactly what happens next.

**Scope:** Every new enquiry by email, website form, phone or social media message, from anyone who might become a customer of Brightline Studio, a 6-person design and marketing services business. It does not cover complaints from existing customers (see SOP 03).

**Who does it:** The studio manager answers enquiries. If the studio manager is away, the lead designer answers them. Nobody else replies to a new enquiry without asking the studio manager first.

### Steps
1. Check the inbox and messages at 9:00 am, 12:30 pm and 3:30 pm every working day. Answer every new enquiry within four working hours.
2. Read the whole enquiry before replying. Note what the customer wants, when they need it, and any budget they mention.
3. Reply in the same channel they used, using their first name and a friendly tone. Thank them for getting in touch.
4. Answer their question directly. If they ask for a price, give a realistic range and say a firm quote follows once we know the details.
5. Ask the three questions we always need: what they need done, when they need it finished, and what their budget range is. Do not ask again if they already answered.
6. Offer a 20-minute introductory call and suggest two specific times in the next three working days. Include the studio manager's phone number.
7. Log the enquiry in the enquiry tracker: date, customer name, what they asked for, and the date of the call or reply sent.
8. If the enquiry is not something we do, say so kindly and suggest one alternative provider if we know a good one. Never leave it unanswered.
9. If there is no reply within five working days, send one short follow-up asking if they still want to talk. Close the enquiry if there is still no reply after five more working days.

**Quality check:** Before sending, read the reply once through. Check the customer's name is spelled correctly, every question has an answer, and the next step is clear. The studio manager checks any reply that promises a price or a deadline.

**If it goes wrong:** If an enquiry sits unanswered for more than one working day, the studio manager calls the customer, apologises briefly, and answers on the call. If we gave a wrong price range, tell the customer honestly and offer the introductory call to work through the correct range.

**Records:** Keep every enquiry and reply in the enquiry tracker: date, customer name and contact details, what they asked for, what we quoted, and the outcome (became a customer, not now, or not a fit).

## SOP 02 — Onboarding a new customer

**Purpose:** To welcome a new customer properly, agree exactly what we will do for them, and get everything we need to start work without delays or confusion.

**Scope:** Every new customer of Brightline Studio once they have accepted a quote and agreed to start, from the accepted quote to the first piece of work being delivered. It does not cover renewing or changing an existing project.

**Who does it:** The studio manager runs the onboarding. The designer who will do the work joins the kick-off call. The customer supplies the information we ask for.

### Steps
1. Send a welcome email within one working day of the customer accepting the quote. Thank them and confirm what we agreed to do, the price, and the delivery date.
2. Attach the written agreement for the customer to sign. It must state the scope, the price, the payment terms (50% deposit, 50% on delivery), and the rounds of changes included (two).
3. Send the deposit invoice with the welcome email. Work does not start until the deposit is paid or the customer confirms in writing that payment is on its way.
4. Send a short starter checklist asking for: logo and brand files, website or social account access if needed, the names of the people we will work with, and examples of work they like.
5. Book a 30-minute kick-off call within five working days of the deposit being paid. Send the call link and a simple agenda: goals, timeline, who does what, and how we will communicate.
6. On the call, agree the customer's single point of contact, weekly updates by email, and the first delivery date.
7. After the call, send a one-page summary email within one working day confirming everything agreed: scope, dates, contacts, and the weekly update day.
8. Create the customer's project folder and save the signed agreement, the paid deposit invoice, the starter checklist, and the kick-off summary in it.
9. Tell the designer the project has started, share the folder, and confirm the first delivery date with them.

**Quality check:** Before the kick-off call, check that the agreement is signed, the deposit is paid, and the starter checklist has been received or chased. If anything is missing, agree it on the call with a date.

**If it goes wrong:** If the customer does not pay the deposit within five working days, the studio manager calls them to check whether there is a problem and offers a new start date. If the customer sends files we cannot open, tell them within one working day and ask for a different format. If the scope changes, write down the change, confirm the new price or date in writing, and get agreement before work starts.

**Records:** Keep the signed agreement, deposit invoice and payment confirmation, starter checklist, kick-off summary email, and project folder for as long as the project runs and for two years after.

## SOP 03 — Handling a complaint

**Purpose:** To deal with every complaint quickly, fairly and calmly, fix what went wrong where we can, and keep the customer's trust even when something has gone wrong.

**Scope:** Any complaint from a customer about our work, service, prices or behaviour, made by email, phone, message or in person. It does not cover a simple request for a change to a project.

**Who does it:** The studio manager handles all complaints. If the complaint is about the studio manager, the lead designer handles it. Nobody argues with a customer or promises compensation without the studio manager agreeing.

### Steps
1. Acknowledge the complaint within four working hours, and never later than the next working day. Thank the customer, say we are sorry they are unhappy, and say we will look into it. Do not blame them or make excuses.
2. Write down what the customer said, the date, and who took the complaint. Save this in the complaints log.
3. Look into what happened within two working days. Talk to the person who did the work, check the project folder, and check emails and messages.
4. Decide how to put it right: redo the work, fix the problem, offer a partial refund, or offer a discount on future work. For anything over $200, tell the whole team first.
5. Contact the customer within three working days with a clear answer: what happened, what we are doing to fix it, and by when. Keep it short and plain.
6. Carry out the fix by the date promised. If we cannot meet that date, tell the customer before the date passes and give a new one.
7. After the fix, ask if the customer is happy with it. If they are, thank them and close the complaint. If not, offer one more fix or a partial refund.
8. If the customer asks for a refund, the studio manager decides. Refunds under $200 can be approved alone; larger refunds need the lead designer to agree as well. Pay any agreed refund within five working days.
9. Once a month, review the complaints log for patterns, such as the same problem happening twice. If a pattern appears, agree a change to stop it happening again.

**Quality check:** Before replying, the studio manager reads the draft and checks that it answers the complaint, apologises where we were at fault, and gives a clear date for the fix. No reply goes out angry or defensive.

**If it goes wrong:** If the customer is still unhappy after two attempts, offer a phone call to talk it through, then a partial refund if the work was genuinely not up to standard. If a complaint is made publicly, reply briefly and politely asking the customer to email us, then handle the rest privately. Never argue in public.

**Records:** Keep the complaint, investigation notes, what was offered, and the final outcome in the complaints log for two years. Do not delete complaint emails or messages.

## SOP 04 — Invoicing and getting paid

**Purpose:** To send every invoice correctly and on time, make it easy for the customer to pay, and know at a glance who has paid and who has not.

**Scope:** All invoices for Brightline Studio: deposits, final payments, and monthly charges, for every customer. It does not cover chasing an overdue invoice (see SOP 05).

**Who does it:** The studio manager prepares and sends all invoices. The lead designer confirms the work is finished before a final invoice is sent.

### Steps
1. Send a deposit invoice of 50% as soon as the customer accepts a quote and signs the agreement. Send the final invoice of 50% on the day the work is delivered, or within two working days.
2. Every invoice must show: our business name and contact details, the customer's name, the invoice number, the date, a clear description of the work, the amount, the tax if any, and the total due.
3. Invoice numbers run in order, starting at 1001, and are never reused. If an invoice is cancelled, write "cancelled" on it and keep it in the file.
4. State the payment terms on every invoice: payment due within 14 days, by bank transfer or card, using the details printed on the invoice.
5. Before sending a final invoice, check with the designer that the work is complete and the customer has received it. Do not invoice for work that has not been delivered.
6. Send the invoice by email as a PDF, with a short message saying what it is for, the amount, and the due date.
7. Record the invoice in the invoice tracker the same day: invoice number, customer, date sent, amount, and due date.
8. When payment arrives, mark the invoice as paid in the tracker the same day, send the customer a short thank-you and receipt, and file the payment confirmation.
9. Check the tracker every Friday. Any invoice due within the next five working days and still unpaid gets a friendly reminder email.

**Quality check:** Before sending, check the amount against the quote, the customer's name, the invoice number, and that the due date is 14 days from the invoice date. The studio manager checks every invoice; no one else sends one.

**If it goes wrong:** If an invoice has the wrong amount, cancel it, issue a corrected invoice with a new number, and email the customer explaining the correction. If a customer says they never received the invoice, resend it the same day and check the email address. If a payment arrives without a reference, match it by amount and date, and ask the customer to confirm if it is still unclear.

**Records:** Keep every invoice, every payment confirmation, and the invoice tracker for seven years. File invoices by number, not by customer, so nothing is missed.

## SOP 05 — Chasing an overdue invoice

**Purpose:** To get overdue payments in politely and firmly, without damaging the relationship, and to know when to stop.

**Scope:** Every invoice past its 14-day due date without being paid in full, for all customers of Brightline Studio. It does not cover invoices not yet due.

**Who does it:** The studio manager chases all overdue invoices. Nobody else contacts a customer about money unless the studio manager asks them to.

### Steps
1. Check the invoice tracker every Monday and list every overdue invoice and how many days overdue it is.
2. The day after the due date, send a short, friendly email: remind the customer of the invoice number, amount, and due date, attach the invoice again, and ask them to let us know if there is a problem.
3. If there is no payment after seven days overdue, send a second email. Be firmer but still polite: say the invoice is a week overdue, ask for payment within five working days, and offer a payment plan if needed.
4. If there is still no payment after 14 days overdue, phone the customer. Ask if they received the invoice, whether the work is a problem, and when they can pay. Agree a payment date and write it down.
5. If the customer agrees a payment date, send a short email confirming it. If they do not pay by that date, phone again the next working day.
6. If the invoice is 30 days overdue with no payment or agreement, send a final notice by email: state the amount, say payment is required within seven days, and further work will be paused until it is paid.
7. Pause all further work for that customer on day 31 if unpaid. Tell them in writing that work is paused and will restart when payment is received.
8. If the invoice is 60 days overdue and unpaid, decide whether to use a debt collection service or write it off. Do not spend more chasing the debt than the invoice is worth.
9. Never threaten, shout, or send more than one reminder daily. Always stay polite and professional, even if the customer is rude.

**Quality check:** Before sending any reminder, check the invoice number, amount, and due date against the tracker. Never chase an invoice already paid — check the bank account first if in doubt.

**If it goes wrong:** If the customer says the work was not satisfactory, pause the chasing and handle it as a complaint (see SOP 03). If they are in financial difficulty, offer to split the payment into two or three monthly parts and put the agreement in writing. If a customer pays only part of the invoice, thank them, update the tracker, and chase the rest on the same schedule.

**Records:** Keep a note of every reminder sent, call made, and promise the customer made, with dates, in the invoice tracker. Keep copies of all reminder emails for seven years.

## SOP 06 — Hiring and onboarding a new team member

**Purpose:** To hire the right person fairly, and to get them settled, trained and productive in their first month.

**Scope:** Hiring for any role at Brightline Studio, from advertising to the end of the new person's first month. It applies to full-time, part-time and freelance hires. It does not cover ending employment.

**Who does it:** The studio manager leads the hiring, and the lead designer helps write the job description and interviews. The team helps welcome the new person.

### Steps
1. Write a one-page job description before advertising: job title, main tasks, skills needed, hours, in-studio or remote, and pay range. The studio manager and lead designer agree it.
2. Advertise on our usual job site and on our social media. Keep the advert open for at least two weeks.
3. Shortlist within five working days of closing and pick the three or four best matches. Reply to everyone who applied within five working days.
4. Interview each shortlisted person for 45 minutes. Ask the same five core questions of everyone, plus questions about past work. Take notes.
5. Ask the best one or two candidates to do a short practical task, such as a design exercise, and give them two working days.
6. Check references for the person we want to hire. Ask a previous employer about their work, reliability and teamwork.
7. Offer the job in writing: title, start date, hours, pay, and a one-month trial period. Give five working days to accept.
8. Before their first day, prepare a desk or remote login, an email address, access to shared files, and a welcome pack with this SOP kit and the team contact list.
9. On the first day, welcome them, show them around (or the online tools), introduce the team, and agree their first week's tasks. Assign a buddy from the team for the first month.
10. In the first week, train them on our tools, file naming, and how we talk to customers. Start with small real tasks, then bigger ones. Hold short check-ins at the end of weeks one, two and four, and agree at week four if the trial is passed.

**Quality check:** Before the offer goes out, check references were taken, the pay matches the budget, and the job description matches the offer. No offer is made on an interview alone.

**If it goes wrong:** If the chosen candidate turns the job down, offer it to the next best within two working days. If the new person is struggling in the first month, talk to them privately, agree extra training or simpler tasks, and review again after two weeks. If nobody suitable applies, rewrite the advert more clearly with a realistic pay range and advertise again.

**Records:** Keep the job description, applications, interview notes, practical task, reference notes, and offer letter for one year. Keep the new starter's signed agreement and training record while they work with us and for two years after.

## SOP 07 — Start-of-day and end-of-day routine

**Purpose:** To start each working day organised and finish it tidy, so nothing gets forgotten, the studio is ready for the next day, and nobody takes work home in their head.

**Scope:** The daily routine for everyone at Brightline Studio on every working day, whether they work in the studio or remotely. It applies to all team members.

**Who does it:** Every team member follows this routine for themselves. The studio manager checks that the shared parts, such as the inbox and the job board, are covered each day.

### Steps
1. Arrive or log on by 9:00 am and check the calendar for meetings, deadlines and calls.
2. Check the shared inbox and your messages. Answer anything urgent — a waiting customer, a deadline today — before other work.
3. Update the shared job board: move finished jobs to "Done" and today's jobs to "In progress". If a job is stuck, note what you are waiting for.
4. Pick the three most important things for today from your task list and write them where you can see them. Start with the hardest or most urgent.
5. If you are waiting on a customer or supplier, send a short chase message before 10:00 am so they have the day to reply.
6. Take a proper lunch break away from your desk. The rota covers the phone and inbox from 12:00 pm to 1:00 pm, so they are never left for more than an hour.
7. At 4:30 pm, stop starting new work. Finish what you are doing, save files with the correct names, and update the job board with what you completed and what is left.
8. Reply to any customer message waiting more than four working hours, or note on the job board that it will be answered first thing tomorrow.
9. Check tomorrow's calendar and note anything to prepare. Leave your desk tidy and log out of shared accounts on a shared computer.
10. If you worked remotely, send a two-line end-of-day message to the team chat: what you finished and tomorrow's plan.

**Quality check:** Before you finish, the job board must match reality: finished jobs marked done, today's messages answered or noted, and tomorrow's top three tasks written down. The studio manager checks the board at 5:00 pm.

**If it goes wrong:** If you are ill or cannot work, tell the studio manager before 9:00 am so your urgent jobs can be covered. If a deadline will be missed, say so as soon as you know, not at the end of the day, so the customer can be told in good time. If the inbox was left uncovered, the studio manager covers it and fixes the rota the next morning.

**Records:** The job board is the record: it shows what was done each day, what is outstanding, and who is doing what. Keep the team chat end-of-day messages; they record progress if a customer asks what happened on a day.

## SOP 08 — Ordering supplies and paying a supplier

**Purpose:** To order what the studio needs without wasting money, check that what arrives is right, and pay suppliers on time so they keep working with us.

**Scope:** Ordering any supplies or services for Brightline Studio, from stationery and printing to software and equipment, and paying the supplier afterwards. It applies to every team member who orders anything.

**Who does it:** Any team member can ask for supplies, but only the studio manager places an order or approves a payment. Orders over $200 also need the lead designer to agree first.

### Steps
1. Check what we already have before ordering — look in the cupboard or check the stock list.
2. For anything over $100, get two prices and pick the better value, not just the cheapest — delivery time and quality count too.
3. The studio manager orders by email so there is a written record. The order must state the items, quantities, price agreed, delivery address, and the date we need it by.
4. Record the order in the supplier tracker the same day: supplier, items, amount, date ordered, and expected delivery date.
5. When the delivery arrives, check it against the order before signing. Count the items, check nothing is damaged, and check the delivery note matches the order.
6. If something is wrong or damaged, tell the supplier the same day by email, with a photo if possible, and ask for a replacement or refund. Do not pay for goods we did not receive or that arrived damaged.
7. When the invoice arrives, check it against the order and delivery note: items, quantities and price must all match. If not, ask the supplier to correct it before paying.
8. Pay the supplier by the date on their invoice, and never later than 30 days after delivery. Record the payment date and method in the supplier tracker.
9. Keep a list of regular suppliers with contact details, what we buy from them, and how reliable they are, so anyone can order when the studio manager is away.

**Quality check:** Before paying any invoice, the studio manager checks three things: the goods arrived, the invoice matches the order, and the price matches what was agreed. If any one of these fails, the payment waits until it is sorted out.

**If it goes wrong:** If a delivery is late, phone the supplier, find out when it will arrive, and tell anyone in the team who is waiting for it. If a supplier sends the wrong items, do not use them; contact them the same day and arrange a return or replacement. If a supplier keeps letting us down, find an alternative supplier and update the supplier list.

**Records:** Keep every order email, delivery note, invoice and payment confirmation in the supplier file for seven years. Keep the supplier tracker up to date so we always know what is on order and owed.

## SOP 09 — Posting on social media

**Purpose:** To post regularly and professionally, so people see our work, trust us, and get in touch — without wasting hours or posting anything embarrassing.

**Scope:** Every post on Brightline Studio's accounts, including photos, videos, stories and replies to comments. It applies to anyone who posts for the business. Personal accounts are not covered.

**Who does it:** The lead designer plans the posts and creates them. The studio manager approves every post before it goes live. Nobody else posts on the business accounts.

### Steps
1. Plan the week's posts every Monday morning. Aim for three a week: one piece of our work, one useful tip, and one behind-the-scenes or team post.
2. Only share a customer's work with written permission. Ask by email and save the reply before creating the post.
3. Write each post in plain, friendly language. Keep it short — two or three sentences. Say what the post is about and end with one clear action, such as "See more of this project on our website."
4. Check every image before use: it must be our own work or a photo we have the right to use, it must look sharp, and it must not show a customer's private information or anyone who has not agreed to appear.
5. Check the spelling, any customer name mentioned, and any link. A broken link or misspelled name looks unprofessional.
6. The studio manager reads and approves every post before it is published. No post goes live without approval.
7. Publish at 9:00 am on weekdays and 11:00 am on Saturdays. Do not post after 6:00 pm or on Sundays unless the studio manager agrees.
8. Check comments and messages twice a day, at 10:00 am and 3:00 pm. Reply to genuine questions and kind comments the same day. Answer new customer enquiries following SOP 01.
9. If someone leaves a rude or unfair comment, do not reply in anger or delete it straight away. Tell the studio manager, who decides whether to reply politely, hide it, or delete it.
10. At the end of each month, note which posts got the most likes, comments and messages. Do more of what worked.

**Quality check:** Before approval, the studio manager checks the spelling, image, link, permission for any customer work shown, and that the post sounds like us — friendly and professional, never boastful or rude.

**If it goes wrong:** If a post goes live with a mistake, correct or delete it within one hour and tell the studio manager. If a post upsets a customer, apologise directly and remove it if they ask. If the account is hacked, change the password immediately, tell the studio manager, and check recent posts.

**Records:** Keep a copy of every post (words and image) and its publish date in the social media folder. Keep customer permissions while the post stays live, and for one year after it is removed.

## SOP 10 — Reporting and fixing a problem (incident)

**Purpose:** To make sure every problem — a mistake, a system failure, a lost file or a security worry — is reported quickly, fixed, and learned from.

**Scope:** Any problem affecting our work, customers or data: lost or deleted files, a computer or software failure, a customer data mistake, a security concern such as a suspicious email, or any accident in the studio. It applies to everyone at Brightline Studio.

**Who does it:** The person who finds the problem reports it. The studio manager decides how to fix it and who does it. Report honestly — nobody gets in trouble for reporting one.

### Steps
1. When you find a problem, ask: is anything at risk? If customer data or money is at risk, tell the studio manager immediately by phone, not just by message.
2. Report it to the studio manager within one hour. Say what happened, when, what you were doing, and who it affects. Do not hide it or fix it secretly.
3. Log it: date and time, who reported it, what happened, and what is affected.
4. Work out how serious it is. Urgent — work stopped, data at risk, or money involved — deal with it today. Less urgent — deal with it within three days.
5. Fix the immediate problem first: restore the file from backup, restart the system, or correct wrong information sent out.
6. If a customer is affected, tell them honestly what happened, what we did, and what happens next — within one working day if it is serious. The studio manager makes this call.
7. Once fixed, work out why it happened: a mistake, a missing step, a broken tool, or something nobody had thought of?
8. Agree one change to stop it happening again, such as a new check or a change to an SOP. Write it down, tell the team, put a date on it, and check after two weeks that it is followed. If the problem happens again, a better change is needed.
9. If personal data was shared wrongly, check whether we need to tell anyone else and get advice if unsure. Do not delay.

**Quality check:** Before closing an incident, check that the fix is done, any affected customer has been told, the cause is known, and a change has been agreed to stop it happening again. Close it only when all four are true.

**If it goes wrong:** If a fix makes things worse, stop, tell the studio manager, and restore from backup or undo the change. If a customer is angry, follow SOP 03 for complaints. If you are unsure whether something is a problem, report it anyway — better to report something small than miss something big.

**Records:** Keep the incident log for two years. For each incident, keep the date, who reported it, what happened, what was done, who was told, and what changed. Keep photos or screenshots of the problem.
