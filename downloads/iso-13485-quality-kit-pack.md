# ISO 13485 Quality Kit
> Ten ready-to-use procedures for a small medical-device maker. Written out in full — no blanks to fill in.
## What's inside
- SOP 01 — Handling a customer complaint
- SOP 02 — Complaint trend review
- SOP 03 — Reporting to the regulator (vigilance)
- SOP 04 — Post-market surveillance plan
- SOP 05 — Field safety corrective action
- SOP 06 — CAPA — fixing a problem for good (corrective and preventive action)
- SOP 07 — Document control
- SOP 08 — Supplier checks
- SOP 09 — Internal audit
- SOP 10 — Training records

**Please note:** These templates help a small manufacturer build its quality system. They are not a guarantee of ISO 13485 certification, and the buyer should get a quality consultant for certification.

**About the example used in this kit:** Every procedure is written out for one invented example business, **Medivance Devices Ltd**, a 5-person company making a simple Class I medical device — a reusable stainless-steel instrument tray used in clinics and hospitals. Replace the company name, product, and job titles with your own. ISO 13485 is the international standard for a quality management system for medical devices.

## SOP 01 — Handling a customer complaint
**Purpose:** To log, investigate, and answer every complaint about the Medivance instrument tray, so no complaint is lost and no faulty tray stays in use.
**Scope:** Every complaint about the tray, by phone, email, letter, or through a distributor — damage, rust, a lid that will not close, sharp edges, missing parts, or a tray that came back dirty after cleaning.
**Who does it:** The Office Manager logs the complaint and sends the first reply. The Quality Lead investigates it. The Managing Director signs off complaints involving injury, patient risk, or a refund or replacement.
### Steps
1. Log the complaint the same working day in the Complaint Log, with the next number (for example, C-2026-014).
2. Record the customer's name, date, contact method, tray model and batch number, how many trays are affected, and what happened, in the customer's own words where possible.
3. Ask for photos and the batch number from the tray label, if not already sent. Keep the photos with the record.
4. Reply within 2 working days, giving the complaint number and saying when we will update them again.
5. If the tray is returned, inspect it. If not, check the photos and the batch record: who made it, what steel was used, and when it was polished and inspected.
6. Write the cause in plain words — for example, "weld bead not polished smooth on batch 214" — not just "manufacturing fault".
7. Decide what to do: replace, repair, or refund the tray, or explain that no fault was found and why. Tell the customer and do it within 10 working days.
8. Search the Complaint Log for the same fault, such as "rust" or "lid". If it has happened before, tell the Quality Lead. It may need a CAPA — a corrective and preventive action, a formal fix that stops the problem coming back (see SOP 06).
9. If the complaint involves an injury or a tray that could have harmed someone, tell the Quality Lead at once. It may need reporting to the regulator (see SOP 03).
10. Close the complaint only when the customer knows the outcome, any replacement or refund has been sent, and the Quality Lead has signed the record.
**Quality check:** Once a month, the Quality Lead checks the Complaint Log against the email inbox and phone message book. Any complaint open over 10 working days is listed for review.
**If it goes wrong:** If a complaint is missed, log it as soon as it is found, note the delay, and tell the customer. If a customer is unhappy with the answer, the Managing Director reviews the case and phones them.
**Records:** Complaint Log entry, emails and photos, investigation notes, batch record copy, and signed closure note. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 02 — Complaint trend review
**Purpose:** To spot patterns in complaints before they become a serious problem, so Medivance fixes a repeating fault instead of answering the same complaint again and again.
**Scope:** All complaints from the last 3 months, and all complaints from the last 12 months once a year. Every tray model and batch.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead prepares the review. The Managing Director and the Production Lead attend the meeting and agree the actions.
### Steps
1. At the end of each month, count that month's complaints by type — rust spots, lid not closing, sharp edge, missing divider, dirty on arrival.
2. Count complaints by batch, and work out the complaint rate: complaints divided by trays sold. Write it plainly, for example, "3 complaints out of 400 trays — 0.75%".
3. Compare this month with the last 3 months. Mark any type up by 2 or more, any batch with 2 or more complaints, and any new type of complaint.
4. Read the details of the complaints that stand out. Look for a shared cause, such as the same steel supplier, the same polishing step, or trays made in the same week.
5. Once every 3 months, hold a 30-minute review meeting with the Managing Director and the Production Lead. The Quality Lead brings the counts, the rates, and a one-page summary.
6. For each pattern, decide: do nothing, watch it for another month, or start a CAPA — a corrective and preventive action, a formal fix that stops the problem coming back (see SOP 06). Write the decision and the reason.
7. If a pattern points to a safety risk — sharp edges that could cut a user, or trays that cannot be cleaned properly — treat it as urgent. Start a CAPA the same day and check whether the regulator needs telling (see SOP 03).
8. Once a year, in January, do a full 12-month review. Compare the year with the year before, list the top 3 complaint types, and set a target, such as "cut rust complaints by half".
9. Write a short Trend Review Report: counts, rates, patterns, decisions, and who does what by when. Everyone at the meeting signs it.
10. File the report with the Complaint Log. Add any new CAPA to the CAPA Log, and check at the next monthly review whether the action worked.
**Quality check:** The Managing Director checks that the monthly count was done on time and the quarterly meeting happened. A missed month is caught up the following week.
**If it goes wrong:** If complaints about one fault jump suddenly — for example, 5 in one week — do not wait for the quarterly meeting. The Quality Lead tells the Managing Director the same day, and the team decides whether to pause sales of that batch.
**Records:** Monthly counts, quarterly Trend Review Reports, annual review report, and meeting notes. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 03 — Reporting to the regulator (vigilance)
**Purpose:** To report serious problems with the tray to the regulator on time, as the law requires. Vigilance is the name for this duty: telling the regulator when a device has caused harm, or could have caused harm.
**Scope:** Any incident with a Medivance tray that caused, or could have caused, death, serious injury, or a serious risk to a patient or user, in any country where it is sold.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead decides whether an incident must be reported and writes the report. The Managing Director reviews and approves it. Nobody else sends a report to the regulator.
### Steps
1. When an incident is reported — by a customer, clinic, distributor, or staff member — log it as a complaint the same day (see SOP 01) and mark it as a possible vigilance case.
2. Gather the facts within 2 working days: what happened, when and where, who was affected, the tray model and batch number, and whether the tray is available for inspection.
3. Decide whether it must be reported. Report it if a patient or user died, was seriously injured, or needed treatment because of the tray, or if the tray failed in a way that could have caused serious harm. When in doubt, report it.
4. Check the deadline for the country where it happened. As a rule, report a death or a serious threat to public health within 2 days, and any other serious incident within 10 days. If the local rule is stricter, follow it.
6. Write the report in plain words: the device, the batch number, what happened, what harm was caused or could have been caused, and what we plan to do. If the cause is not yet known, say "cause under investigation".
7. The Managing Director reads and signs the report first. Send it through the regulator's reporting system or email address, and save the confirmation receipt.
8. If the investigation finds new facts — a different cause, more affected trays, or another incident — send a follow-up report, marked as a follow-up with the original reference.
9. If trays need recalling, repairing, or replacing, start a field safety corrective action at once (see SOP 05). The regulator report and the field safety action go together.
10. Close the case only when the regulator has acknowledged the report, the investigation is finished, and any corrective action is complete.
**Quality check:** The Quality Lead checks every open vigilance case each week until it is closed, so no deadline is missed.
**If it goes wrong:** If a deadline is missed, send the report at once and note the delay and the reason. If an incident was wrongly judged as not reportable, report it now, record the mistake, and start a CAPA (see SOP 06).
**Records:** Incident log, investigation notes, reports, regulator receipts, follow-up reports, and closure note. Keep for at least 5 years, or longer if the regulator requires it.
## SOP 04 — Post-market surveillance plan
**Purpose:** To keep watching the tray after it is sold, so Medivance finds problems in real use and keeps the tray safe. Post-market surveillance means checking how a device performs once customers are using it, not only before sale.
**Scope:** The reusable instrument tray, in every country where it is sold. It covers complaints, returns, feedback from distributors and clinics, and safety information about similar trays.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead owns the plan and writes the yearly report. The Office Manager collects feedback. The Managing Director approves the plan and the report.
### Steps
1. Once a year, in January, the Quality Lead writes or updates the Post-Market Surveillance Plan. It lists what information will be collected, who collects it, and how often it is reviewed.
2. The plan covers: customer complaints (see SOP 01), complaint trends (see SOP 02), returned trays and warranty claims, distributor feedback, clinic comments, and regulator safety notices about similar trays.
3. For each source, write how it is collected. For example, complaints come from the Complaint Log, distributor feedback from a short email sent every 6 months, and regulator notices are checked monthly.
4. Set a simple target for the year, such as "no more than 1 complaint per 200 trays sold" and "zero serious incidents". Write it in the plan.
5. During the year, the Office Manager files every piece of feedback as it arrives, with the date and the source. The Quality Lead checks the file once a month.
6. Every 3 months, the Quality Lead reviews the feedback: are complaints going up or down, is any new problem appearing, and is any source missing? Note the findings in a short quarterly note.
7. If the review finds a new risk — for example, trays rusting faster where a hospital uses a new cleaning chemical — start a CAPA (see SOP 06) and, if needed, report to the regulator (see SOP 03).
8. At the end of the year, write the Surveillance Report: trays sold, complaints by type, feedback received, whether the targets were met, and what actions were taken or are planned.
9. The Managing Director reads and signs the report, and agrees the actions for the next year. If the tray needs a design or process change, raise it through document control (see SOP 07).
10. File the plan and the report together, and use the report to update next year's plan.
**Quality check:** The Managing Director checks that the quarterly notes were written and the yearly report was finished by the end of January. A missed quarter is caught up within 2 weeks.
**If it goes wrong:** If a source of feedback stops arriving, the Office Manager contacts them and the Quality Lead notes the gap. If the yearly targets were missed, start a CAPA to find out why.
**Records:** Surveillance Plan, quarterly notes, yearly Surveillance Report, feedback file, and regulator notice checks. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 05 — Field safety corrective action
**Purpose:** To take fast, organised action when a tray already sold turns out to be unsafe or faulty. A field safety corrective action is a recall, repair, replacement, or safety warning for devices already out in the world.
**Scope:** Every tray affected by the fault, in every country where it was sold, including trays held by distributors.
**Who does it:** The Managing Director decides to start the action and is in charge of it. The Quality Lead runs the investigation and writes the notices. The Office Manager contacts customers and tracks returns.
### Steps
1. Start the same day the problem is found — from a complaint, a trend review, an audit, or a regulator notice — if the fault could cause harm.
2. The Quality Lead and the Managing Director meet the same day and agree the scope: which tray models, which batch numbers, and how many trays are affected. Use sales and batch records to work out the numbers.
3. Decide what to do: ask customers to stop using the trays and return them, send a replacement, repair them, or give new instructions for safe use. If in doubt, stop use first.
4. Tell the regulator before contacting customers, or at the same time if the risk is urgent (see SOP 03). Write down the regulator's reference number.
5. Write a Field Safety Notice in plain words: the tray, the batches affected, the problem, what harm could happen, what customers should do now, and who to contact. The Managing Director signs it first.
6. Send the notice to every affected customer and distributor by email and by post, and phone the largest customers the same day. Keep a list of everyone contacted.
7. Track the response: how many trays have been returned, replaced, or confirmed as not in use. Chase anyone who has not replied within 5 working days, and again after 10 working days.
8. When a tray is returned, inspect it, log it, and decide whether it can be repaired, must be scrapped, or needs further testing. Never resell a returned tray without a full check.
9. Once all affected trays are accounted for, or a reasonable effort has been made to reach every customer, write a closing report: what was done, how many trays were recovered, and what will stop the fault happening again (see SOP 06).
10. The Managing Director signs the closing report and sends a copy to the regulator if asked.
**Quality check:** The Quality Lead checks the contact list every week until every customer has replied or been chased twice.
**If it goes wrong:** If a customer cannot be reached, note every attempt and keep trying through the distributor. If more affected batches are found after the notice goes out, send an updated notice at once and tell the regulator.
**Records:** Investigation notes, regulator correspondence, Field Safety Notice, contact list, returns record, and closing report. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 06 — CAPA — fixing a problem for good (corrective and preventive action)
**Purpose:** To fix the real cause of a problem so it does not come back, and to stop a problem that has not happened yet. CAPA stands for corrective and preventive action: a corrective action fixes a problem that has happened; a preventive action stops one that could happen.
**Scope:** Any repeating complaint, serious incident, audit finding, failed inspection, or trend that shows a risk.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead owns the CAPA and writes it up. The Production Lead carries out the fix on the shop floor. The Managing Director approves the CAPA and checks that it worked.
### Steps
1. Start a CAPA when a problem repeats, a trend review calls for one (see SOP 02), an audit finds a problem (see SOP 09), or a serious incident happens. Log it the same day.
2. Write the problem in plain words: what happened, when, and how many trays were affected. Attach the complaint records, photos, or audit notes.
3. Find the root cause — the real reason underneath, not just the symptom. Ask "why" at least 3 times. Example: trays rusted because steel was stored damp; the steel was damp because the roof leaked; the roof leaked because it had not been checked in 2 years.
4. Write the root cause down and agree it with the Managing Director. If the cause is not clear, do more checking first.
5. Choose the action that removes the root cause: a process change, a new check, a different supplier, new training, or a design change. Write what will be done and who will do it, and by when.
6. Carry out the action. If it is a process change, update the procedure first (see SOP 07) and train staff on the new way of working (see SOP 10) before it goes live.
7. Check that the fix worked. Wait at least a month, or until at least 50 trays have been made the new way, then check the complaint log or inspection records. Write down what the check showed.
8. If the problem is gone, the Managing Director signs the CAPA closed. If it is still there, go back to step 3 and try a different fix. Never close a CAPA that has not worked.
9. Once a month, the Quality Lead reviews the CAPA Log: how many are open, how many are overdue, and how many were closed. Overdue CAPAs are reported to the Managing Director.
**Quality check:** No CAPA is closed until the check in step 7 shows the fix worked.
**If it goes wrong:** If a fix does not work, do not just try the same fix harder. Reopen the investigation and record what was tried and why it failed.
**Records:** CAPA Log, CAPA form with the problem, root cause, action, and effectiveness check, plus updated procedures and training records. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 07 — Document control
**Purpose:** To make sure everyone at Medivance uses the current version of every procedure, form, and drawing, and that old versions cannot be used by mistake.
**Scope:** All quality documents: these SOPs, work instructions, forms, tray drawings, cleaning instructions, and the quality manual. Not general business papers such as invoices.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead controls all documents and keeps the master list. The Managing Director approves new documents and changes. All staff use only the current versions.
### Steps
1. Every quality document has a title, a document number (for example, SOP-01), a version number, a date, and the name of the person who approved it. The version number goes up by one each time the document changes.
2. The Quality Lead keeps a Document Master List showing every document, its current version, its date, and where the master copy is kept. Master copies live in the Quality Folder on the office computer, backed up weekly.
3. When a document needs to change, anyone can suggest it, but the Quality Lead writes the change. Describe it in a short note: what changed, why, and which steps are affected.
4. The Managing Director reviews and approves the change before it takes effect. For a change to the tray design or how it is made, the Quality Lead also checks whether it affects safety or the regulator's requirements.
5. Once approved, the Quality Lead issues the new version: update the version number and date, mark the old version "Superseded", and put the new version in the Quality Folder and on the shop floor if needed.
6. Tell all staff about the change the same day, in person or by email. Collect old printed copies and destroy them or mark them as superseded. Only the current version stays on the shop floor.
7. Train staff on the changed document if it affects their work (see SOP 10). Record the training before the new version is used.
8. Control forms the same way. When a form changes, throw away the old blank forms, so nobody fills in an old form by mistake.
9. Once a year, the Quality Lead reviews the Master List with the Managing Director, checking that every document is still current, still needed, and still in use. Documents no longer needed are marked "Withdrawn" and filed away.
**Quality check:** During the internal audit (see SOP 09), the auditor checks that the shop floor has only current versions. Any old version found is recorded as an audit finding.
**If it goes wrong:** If an old version is found in use, take it away at once, check whether any work done with it needs redoing, and record what happened. If a document is lost, remake it from the backup and note the loss.
**Records:** Document Master List, approved and superseded versions, change notes, and the yearly review note. Keep superseded versions for at least 5 years.
## SOP 08 — Supplier checks
**Purpose:** To make sure the steel, packaging, and services Medivance buys are good enough for a medical device, and that suppliers who let us down are dealt with before a bad batch reaches a customer.
**Scope:** Every supplier whose goods or services affect the tray: steel, welding consumables, packaging, cleaning chemicals, and the outside polishing and calibration services.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead approves suppliers and checks their goods. The Office Manager places orders and keeps supplier records. The Managing Director approves any new supplier.
### Steps
1. Keep an Approved Supplier List. No supplier is used for anything that affects the tray until they are on the list. The list shows the supplier name, what they supply, and the date approved.
2. Before approving a new supplier, ask for a certificate for the steel grade, a product specification, and, where possible, a sample. For steel, the certificate must show the grade and batch number, and that it meets the grade specification.
3. For critical work, such as outside polishing or calibration, ask for evidence they can do the work well — their own quality certificate, references, or a visit to their premises.
4. The Managing Director approves the supplier, and the Quality Lead adds them to the Approved Supplier List with the date and the reason for approval.
5. When goods arrive, check them before they are used: the right goods, the right quantity, no visible damage, and the paperwork matches the order. For steel, check the certificate matches the batch number.
6. Write the check in the Goods-In Log: date, supplier, what arrived, batch number, certificate number, and whether it passed or failed. Steel that fails is marked "Hold" with a red tag and kept apart from good stock.
7. If goods fail, tell the supplier the same day, keep the failed goods separate, and do not use them. Record the failure in the supplier's file. Two failures from one supplier in 6 months triggers a review of that supplier.
8. Once a year, the Quality Lead reviews every supplier: how many deliveries they made, how many failed, whether they delivered on time, and whether any complaints were linked to their goods.
9. After the review, decide for each supplier: keep them approved, approve them with conditions (for example, check every delivery), or remove them from the list. If a supplier is removed, find a replacement before stock runs out.
**Quality check:** The Quality Lead checks the Goods-In Log once a month to make sure every delivery was checked and logged.
**If it goes wrong:** If goods from an unapproved supplier were used by mistake, tell the Quality Lead at once. Check the trays made from those goods, and if any is affected, treat it as a complaint or a CAPA (see SOP 01 and SOP 06).
**Records:** Approved Supplier List, approval records, certificates, Goods-In Log, yearly review notes, and supplier files. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 09 — Internal audit
**Purpose:** To check, by looking at the evidence, whether Medivance is following its own procedures and whether the quality system is working — before a customer, a regulator, or a certification body finds the gaps.
**Scope:** All 10 procedures in this kit, all records, the shop floor, and the office. The whole system is audited at least once a year.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead plans the audits. The auditor is someone who does not do the work being audited — usually the Managing Director audits production and the Quality Lead audits the office.
### Steps
1. At the start of each year, the Quality Lead writes an Audit Plan: which areas will be audited, in which month, and who will audit them. The plan covers every procedure at least once in the year.
2. Before each audit, the auditor reads the procedure for the area and the records from the last 3 months, and writes a short checklist.
3. The auditor visits the area, watches the work, asks staff how they do it, and looks at the records. For example, pick 3 recent complaints and follow each one from log to closure.
4. Write down what was found as you go: what was done well, and anything that does not match the procedure. Each problem is a finding, with the evidence — for example, "Complaint C-2026-011 had no reply within 2 working days; the log shows the reply went out on day 6."
5. At the end of the audit, the auditor talks through the findings with the person in charge, so there are no surprises in the report.
6. Within 5 working days, the auditor writes the Audit Report: the date, the area audited, who was there, what was checked, and the findings. The report goes to the Managing Director and the Quality Lead.
7. For each finding, the Quality Lead raises a CAPA (see SOP 06) or a simple correction, with a named person and a date. Small fixes are done within 2 weeks; bigger fixes get a CAPA.
8. The auditor, or the Quality Lead, checks that each fix was done and that it worked, and signs the finding closed. A finding is not closed on a promise — only when the evidence shows the fix is in place.
9. Once a year, the Quality Lead writes a one-page summary of all audits: how many were done, how many findings were raised, and how many are still open. The Managing Director reviews it and uses it to plan improvements.
**Quality check:** The Managing Director checks that the Audit Plan was followed and no audit was skipped.
**If it goes wrong:** If an audit finds a serious problem, the Managing Director is told the same day and the work is stopped until it is fixed.
**Records:** Audit Plan, checklists, Audit Reports, Audit Findings Log, and yearly summary. Keep for at least 5 years.
## SOP 10 — Training records
**Purpose:** To make sure everyone at Medivance knows how to do their job properly, and to prove it with a record. In a 5-person company, one untrained person can affect every tray that goes out.
**Scope:** All staff, including new starters, part-time staff, and anyone back after a long absence. It covers training on these SOPs, tray-making, cleaning and inspection, and any new equipment.
**Who does it:** The Quality Lead keeps the training records and checks they are complete. The Managing Director, the Production Lead, or the Quality Lead delivers the training.
### Steps
1. Keep a Training Matrix — a list showing every member of staff, every job they need training in, and the date they were last trained. The Quality Lead updates it whenever someone joins, changes job, or finishes training.
2. When a new person starts, the Managing Director gives them an induction on their first day: a walk around the workshop, the safety rules, where the procedures are kept, and who to ask.
3. Before anyone does a job on their own — polishing a tray, inspecting a finished tray, or logging a complaint — they are trained by someone who already does that job well. The trainer shows them, watches them do it, and signs them off only when they can do it without help.
4. Record training on a Training Record for each person and topic: name, topic, date, trainer, and both signatures. A signature means "I was trained and I understand it".
5. When a procedure changes (see SOP 07), everyone affected is trained on the new version before they use it. The Quality Lead checks the records before the new version goes live.
6. Some jobs need refresher training once a year: inspection of finished trays, complaint handling, and reporting to the regulator. The Quality Lead reminds staff a month before it is due.
7. Keep a record of outside training too, such as a course on auditing or on ISO 13485. File the certificate in the person's training file.
8. If someone has not done a job for more than 6 months, they are retrained before they do it again.
9. Once a year, the Quality Lead checks the Training Matrix against the training files. Any gaps are listed, and the missing training is booked within a month.
**Quality check:** The internal audit (see SOP 09) checks a sample of training records against the jobs people actually do. Anyone doing a job without a record is an audit finding.
**If it goes wrong:** If someone is found doing a job without training, stop them until they are trained, and check the trays they made. If a record is missing, the trainer and trainee sign a note saying when the training happened — do not backdate a record.
**Records:** Training Matrix, Training Records, induction records, and outside course certificates. Keep while the person works at Medivance, and after they leave as long as the law requires.
