# Childcare SOP Pack
> Ten ready-to-use procedures for a small daycare. Written out in full — no blanks to fill in.
>
> Please note: these are record-keeping and routine templates for running a small daycare. They are not legal advice. Every childcare setting must check its own local childcare regulations, licensing rules, and safeguarding requirements, and adapt these procedures to fit them before use.

## SOP 01 — Morning opening and room safety check
**Purpose:** To make sure Little Acorns Daycare is clean, safe, and ready before the first child arrives.
**Scope:** This procedure covers the whole building — the two playrooms, the sleep room, the kitchen, the toilets and nappy-changing area, the hallway, the entrance, and the outdoor play area — every morning before opening.
**Who does it:** The opening member of staff, who arrives at 7:30 am. A second member of staff checks the outdoor area and signs the checklist as well. The manager reviews it by 9:00 am.
### Steps
1. Unlock the front door, switch on the lights, and check that the heating is on and the rooms feel warm and comfortable, not cold or stuffy.
2. Walk through every room and look for anything out of place: water on the floor, broken toys, loose cords, or items left from the day before. Clear anything unsafe straight away.
3. Check that all plug sockets have covers fitted, that cleaning products and medicines are locked away, and that the first-aid box is in its usual place, sealed, and in date.
4. Check the toilets and sinks: flush each toilet, run the taps, and make sure there is soap, paper towels, and toilet paper. Report any leak or blockage to the manager before children arrive.
5. Check the nappy-changing area: clean mat, fresh gloves, nappy bags, wipes, and a clean towel. Wipe the mat with the cleaning spray and leave it to dry.
6. Set out the morning activities: place toys and books on low shelves, and remove any toy with a broken part or a sharp edge.
7. Check the kitchen: clean worktops, fridge temperature in the safe range shown on the fridge thermometer, fresh drinking water ready, and no food left out from the day before.
8. Check the outdoor play area: look for litter, animal mess, broken fencing, or wet and slippery surfaces. Lock the gate and check that it closes properly. Do not let children outside until this check is done.
9. Check the fire exits are clear, the entrance mat is flat, and the sign-in book and pen are on the desk by the door.
10. Complete the daily opening checklist, sign it, and note anything that needs fixing. Tell the manager about any problem that cannot be fixed before opening time.
**Quality check:** The manager reads the opening checklist each morning and checks one room at random. Any room that fails is not used until the problem is fixed.
**If it goes wrong:** If something is broken or unsafe and cannot be fixed before opening, close off that room, tell the manager at once, and move the children to a safe room. If the building cannot be made safe, the manager contacts parents and does not open.
**Records:** Daily opening checklist, signed by the opening member of staff and the second checker, kept in the office folder for one year.
## SOP 02 — Child arrival and sign-in
**Purpose:** To welcome every child safely, to know exactly which children are in the building, and to pass on important information from parents before the day begins.
**Scope:** This procedure covers every child arriving between 8:00 am and 9:30 am, including late arrivals, children dropped off by someone other than a parent, and children who arrive upset or unwell.
**Who does it:** The member of staff on door duty greets families and supervises sign-in. The child's key worker takes over once the child is settled. The manager is called if a child arrives unwell or the person dropping off is not known.
### Steps
1. Greet the child and the parent by name at the door. Make the child feel welcome, and take a moment to notice how the child looks and feels that morning.
2. Ask the parent to sign the child in, writing the child's name, the exact arrival time, and the name of the person dropping off. Check the time is correct before the parent leaves.
3. Ask the parent about anything important: a bad night's sleep, a cough or temperature, a medicine to give, a different person collecting, or a change in routine. Write it on the daily handover sheet.
4. Check the child's bag with the parent: spare clothes, nappies, comfort toy, and any food or medicine. Label anything unlabelled, and put medicines straight into the locked medicine box, never in the bag.
5. Help the child take off their coat and shoes, wash their hands, and settle into an activity. Stay close for the first few minutes, especially if the child is upset.
6. If a child arrives after 9:30 am, sign them in as a late arrival, tell the key worker, and check whether breakfast or the morning snack has been served so the child does not miss out.
7. If the person dropping off is not on the child's authorised list, do not accept the child until the parent has confirmed the arrangement by phone. Record the call.
8. Count the children in each room after every arrival and update the room register, so the number on paper always matches the number present.
9. If a child seems unwell — hot, pale, very sleepy, or with a rash — take their temperature, keep them comfortable, and call the manager.
10. At 9:30 am, the room leader checks the sign-in book against the room register and files the handover sheet.
**Quality check:** The manager checks the sign-in book and registers at 10:00 am. Every child present must appear in both, with a matching arrival time.
**If it goes wrong:** If a signed-in child cannot be found, stop, count every child again, and search calmly. Tell the manager at once. If a parent leaves without signing in, call them that morning and complete the record.
**Records:** Daily sign-in book, room registers, and daily handover sheet, kept in the office for one year.
## SOP 03 — Child collection and authorised pickups
**Purpose:** To make sure every child leaves only with a person authorised to collect them, and that the handover is calm, clear, and properly recorded.
**Scope:** This procedure covers all collections, including normal end-of-day pickup, early collection, collection by grandparents or other authorised adults, and collection when a parent is delayed.
**Who does it:** The member of staff on door duty checks who is collecting and supervises sign-out. The child's key worker gives the handover. The manager decides what happens if an unauthorised person arrives.
### Steps
1. When a person arrives to collect a child, greet them by name and check them against the authorised pickup list, kept in the child's file and on the wall by the door.
2. Ask for photo identification if the person is not known to staff, even if they say they are family. Do not hand over a child to anyone you cannot verify.
3. If the person is not on the authorised list, do not release the child. Keep the child with staff, call the parent, and wait for clear permission.
4. Ask the person collecting to sign the child out, writing the child's name, the exact time, and their own name. Check the time is correct.
5. Give a short, friendly handover: what the child ate, how long they slept, anything they enjoyed, and anything the parent should know, such as a bump or a change in mood.
6. Help the child get their coat, shoes, bag, and any artwork or belongings. Check the child's bag together so nothing is left behind.
7. If a parent is running late, reassure the child, keep them with staff, and try the parent's phone. If the parent cannot be reached after 30 minutes, call the emergency contacts in order.
8. If a person arrives who seems upset, unwell, or under the influence of alcohol or drugs, do not hand over the child. Keep the child safe, stay calm, and call the manager at once.
9. At the end of the day, the room leader counts the children left and checks the sign-out book, so the number still present is always known.
10. If a child has not been collected by closing time, stay with the child, and record every call made and its time.
**Quality check:** The manager checks the sign-out book each evening. Every child who arrived must have a matching sign-out entry with a time and a collector's name.
**If it goes wrong:** If a child is collected by the wrong person, or leaves without being signed out, tell the manager immediately and call the parent straight away. Write down exactly what happened and when. The manager reviews the procedure with all staff the next day.
**Records:** Daily sign-out book, authorised pickup lists in each child's file, and a record of any late collection or refused collection, kept in the office for one year.
## SOP 04 — Nappy changing and hygiene
**Purpose:** To change nappies safely, hygienically, and respectfully, so children stay clean and comfortable and the risk of spreading germs stays low.
**Scope:** This procedure covers all nappy changes, for every child who wears nappies, at any time of day, including after meals, after sleep, and whenever a child is wet or soiled.
**Who does it:** The child's key worker, or another trained member of staff if the key worker is busy. Nappy changes are never done by students, volunteers, or visitors.
### Steps
1. Wash your hands with soap and water, and put on a fresh pair of disposable gloves before you start.
2. Collect everything you need first: a clean nappy, wipes, a nappy bag, and a change of clothes if needed. Never leave a child alone on the changing mat, even for a moment.
3. Talk to the child calmly throughout, explaining what you are doing in simple words, so the child feels safe.
4. Lay the child gently on the mat, remove the used nappy, and put it straight into a nappy bag. Tie the bag and place it in the nappy bin.
5. Clean the child's skin carefully with wipes, front to back, using a fresh wipe for each stroke. Check for redness, soreness, or a rash.
6. If the skin is sore, apply the child's own barrier cream, if the parent has supplied it and signed the cream form. Never use another child's cream.
7. Put on the clean nappy, making sure it fits snugly but not tightly, and dress the child. Check that clothes are clean and comfortable.
8. Wash the child's hands, or wipe them with a clean damp cloth if the child is very young, and help them back to play.
9. Remove your gloves, put them in the nappy bin, wash your hands thoroughly, and wipe the mat with cleaning spray. Leave it to dry before the next use.
10. Record the change on the child's daily nappy chart, noting the time and whether the nappy was wet, soiled, or dry, and note any soreness so the key worker can tell the parent.
**Quality check:** The room leader checks the changing area twice a day to make sure it is clean and stocked. The manager spot-checks nappy charts each week.
**If it goes wrong:** If a child has a rash or an unusual stool, note it on the chart, tell the key worker, and mention it to the parent at pickup. If a child has diarrhoea, keep them comfortable and call the parent to collect them. Clean and disinfect the area thoroughly.
**Records:** Daily nappy chart for each child, and the barrier cream consent form in the child's file, kept for one year.
## SOP 05 — Meals, snacks and allergies
**Purpose:** To serve safe, healthy food, to protect every child with an allergy or special diet, and to make mealtimes calm and enjoyable.
**Scope:** This procedure covers breakfast, lunch, morning and afternoon snacks, and drinks, including food brought from home, birthday treats, and food used in cooking activities.
**Who does it:** The cook prepares food and checks allergy lists. The room leader checks the allergy board before every meal. All staff serving food must know the allergies in their room.
### Steps
1. Before any meal, check the allergy board in the kitchen and in each room. It lists every child's name, their allergy, and what they must not be given.
2. Wash your hands, and help the children wash theirs, before food is served. Clean tables and high chairs with the food-safe spray and lay out clean cups, plates, and cutlery.
3. Serve food that matches each child's needs. Children with allergies eat food prepared separately, on a different coloured plate where possible.
4. Check the temperature of hot food before serving, and cut food into small, safe pieces for younger children. Never serve whole grapes, whole nuts, or hard sweets.
5. Sit with the children while they eat. Encourage them to try foods, talk with them, and watch for any child who is struggling or coughing.
6. Offer water with every meal and snack, and keep water available all day. Do not offer fizzy drinks.
7. If a child brings food from home, check it against their allergy record, label it, and store it in the fridge or lunchbox area. Do not share food between children.
8. Clear away promptly: scrape plates into the food waste bin, wash up or load the dishwasher, wipe tables and high chairs, and sweep the floor.
9. Record what each child ate on the daily food sheet, noting if a child ate very little or refused a meal, so parents can see it at pickup.
10. If a child has an allergic reaction — swelling, rash, sickness, or trouble breathing — act at once, follow the child's allergy plan, and call for help and the emergency services if the symptoms are serious. Tell the manager and the parent immediately.
**Quality check:** The manager checks the allergy board against the children's files every month and after any new allergy is reported. The cook checks fridge temperatures daily.
**If it goes wrong:** If the wrong food is served, stay calm, watch the child closely for signs of a reaction, and call the manager. If there are symptoms, follow the child's allergy plan and call for medical help. Record exactly what happened.
**Records:** Allergy board, children's allergy records in their files, daily food sheets, and fridge temperature sheets, kept in the office and kitchen for one year.
## SOP 06 — Sleep and rest time
**Purpose:** To give every child safe, comfortable rest, so tired children can sleep in a calm room where they are always watched.
**Scope:** This procedure covers midday sleep and rest time for all children, including babies who sleep more than once a day, children who rest but do not sleep, and children who wake early.
**Who does it:** The sleep room worker settles the children and stays in the room for the whole rest period. The room leader checks the room every 15 minutes.
### Steps
1. Prepare the sleep room before rest time: clean bedding, a cot or sleep mat for each child, a comfortable temperature, curtains partly drawn, and soft lighting.
2. Help each child settle in their usual place, with their own comfort toy or blanket if they have one. Keep the same places each day where possible.
3. Change nappies and help children use the toilet before rest time, so they are clean and dry when they lie down.
4. Stay in the sleep room for the whole rest period. Sit where you can see every child, and keep the room quiet.
5. Check every sleeping child every 10 minutes: look at their breathing, colour, and position. Make sure no blanket covers a baby's face and babies are lying on their backs.
6. Write down the time each child falls asleep and wakes, on the daily sleep chart, so parents know how long their child rested.
7. If a child does not fall asleep after 20 minutes, let them rest quietly with a book, then take them back to the playroom. Do not force a child to stay lying down if they are wide awake.
8. Wake children gently at the end of rest time. Give them a few minutes to wake up, offer water, and help them get dressed.
9. Tidy the sleep room after rest time: fold blankets, stack mats, open the curtains, and air the room.
10. Tell the key worker if a child slept much longer or shorter than usual, or seemed unwell during sleep, so the parent can be told at pickup.
**Quality check:** The room leader checks the sleep charts each day and visits the sleep room unannounced during rest time. The manager reviews sleep records each month.
**If it goes wrong:** If a child seems unwell during sleep — breathing oddly, very hot, very pale, or hard to wake — stay with the child, call for help at once, and call the emergency services if needed. Record exactly what happened and tell the parent straight away.
**Records:** Daily sleep chart for each child, kept in the office for one year.
## SOP 07 — Accidents and incident reporting
**Purpose:** To deal with accidents quickly and calmly, to give the right first aid, to tell parents honestly what happened, and to record every accident so the daycare can learn from it.
**Scope:** This procedure covers every accident, bump, fall, bite, or injury, whether indoors or outdoors, and whether the injury is small or serious.
**Who does it:** The first member of staff to see the accident gives first aid. A trained first aider takes over if the injury needs more than a plaster. The manager is called for any head injury or serious injury.
### Steps
1. Stay calm, go to the child quickly, and comfort them. Sit them down safely and check what happened and where it hurts.
2. Give first aid: clean a graze with water, apply a plaster, or hold a cold compress on a bump. Wear gloves if there is any blood.
3. If the injury is serious — a deep cut, a suspected broken bone, a head injury with vomiting or drowsiness — call the first aider and the manager at once, and call the emergency services if needed.
4. Keep the child still and comfortable while help arrives. Do not move a child who may have hurt their neck or back.
5. Keep the other children calm and safe. Ask another member of staff to look after the group.
6. Once the child is comfortable, fill in the accident form: the child's name, date and time, where it happened, what happened, what first aid was given, and who was present.
7. Ask the member of staff who saw the accident to sign the form, and ask a witness to sign it as well if one was there.
8. Tell the parent at pickup, or by phone straight away if the injury is serious. Show them the accident form, explain what happened plainly, and ask them to sign it.
9. Give the parent a copy of the form if they ask for one, and file the original in the child's file.
10. At the end of the week, the manager reads all accident forms together and looks for patterns — the same place, toy, or time of day — and fixes the cause if one can be found.
**Quality check:** The manager checks every accident form on the day it is written, to make sure it is complete and signed. Any unclear form is sent back the same day.
**If it goes wrong:** If a parent is upset, listen calmly, explain what happened honestly, and offer to go through the form together. If a child needs hospital treatment, a member of staff goes with them and stays until the parent arrives.
**Records:** Accident forms in each child's file, and a weekly accident summary kept by the manager, kept for at least three years.
## SOP 08 — Giving medicine
**Purpose:** To give medicine safely, only when parents have asked for it in writing, only in the right dose, and only with a clear record of every dose given.
**Scope:** This procedure covers all medicine given, including prescribed medicine such as antibiotics and inhalers, and non-prescribed medicine such as children's pain relief, when parents have asked for it in writing.
**Who does it:** A trained member of staff gives the medicine, and a second member watches and signs the form. The manager checks the forms each week. No medicine is ever given by a student, volunteer, or visitor.
### Steps
1. Check that the parent has signed the consent form, which states the child's name, the medicine, the dose, the times it should be given, and the last date it may be given.
2. Check the medicine: the child's name on the label, the expiry date, and that it matches the form. Do not give medicine that is out of date, unlabelled, or in a different child's name.
3. Store all medicine in the locked medicine box, out of children's reach. Keep medicine that needs to stay cold in the fridge, in a labelled box.
4. Wash your hands before giving medicine, and read the label and consent form again, out loud, with the second member of staff listening.
5. Measure the dose carefully using the spoon, syringe, or cup that came with the medicine. Never guess a dose, and never give more than the written dose.
6. Give the medicine to the child calmly, in a quiet place, and stay with the child until you are sure they have swallowed it.
7. If the child refuses the medicine or spits it out, do not try again on your own. Tell the manager and call the parent for advice.
8. Record the dose straight away on the medicine form: the child's name, the medicine, the dose, the exact time, and both staff names. Both staff sign.
9. Watch the child after the medicine is given, and note any change — a rash, sickness, or drowsiness — and tell the manager.
10. Give the medicine form to the parent at pickup, explain what was given and when, and ask them to sign it. File the signed form in the child's file.
**Quality check:** The manager checks all medicine forms each Friday, to make sure every dose has two signatures and the right times and doses. Any missing signature is followed up the same day.
**If it goes wrong:** If the wrong medicine or dose is given, stay calm, do not give any more, and tell the manager at once. Call the parent and, if needed, a doctor or the emergency services for advice. Write down exactly what happened and when.
**Records:** Medicine consent forms and medicine administration forms in each child's file, kept for at least three years.
## SOP 09 — Fire drill and evacuation
**Purpose:** To get every child and every member of staff out quickly and safely if there is a fire or alarm, and to know everyone is accounted for.
**Scope:** This procedure covers fire drills and real evacuations, at any time of day, including sleep time and outdoor play, and for every person in the building.
**Who does it:** The manager, or the senior member of staff on duty, is the fire warden and leads the evacuation. Every member of staff is responsible for the children in their room.
### Steps
1. When the fire alarm sounds, or a fire is found, stay calm. Do not stop to collect bags or coats.
2. The fire warden calls the emergency services, or asks another member of staff to do so, giving the name and address.
3. Each room leader gathers the children, counts them, and leads them out by the nearest safe exit. Hold younger children's hands, and carry babies and children who cannot walk quickly.
4. Close doors behind you as you leave, if it is safe to do so, to slow smoke and fire. Do not open a door that feels hot.
5. Take the room register and sign-in book with you if they are close at hand. Do not go back in for them.
6. Walk, do not run, to the assembly point in the front car park, well away from the building. Keep the children together.
7. At the assembly point, each room leader counts their children against the register. The fire warden counts the staff.
8. If anyone is missing, tell the fire warden at once. Nobody goes back in to look for them — the fire warden tells the emergency services.
9. Wait at the assembly point until the fire warden says it is safe to go back inside. Keep the children calm and together.
10. After the drill or a real evacuation, the fire warden writes down how long it took, whether everyone was accounted for, and anything that went wrong. The manager reviews it and fixes any problems.
**Quality check:** The daycare holds a fire drill at least once every three months, at different times of day, including once during sleep time. The manager reviews the record and checks the evacuation time stays within a safe range.
**If it goes wrong:** If an exit is blocked, use the next nearest safe exit and tell the fire warden. If a child is frightened and will not move, pick them up and carry them out. If the alarm fails, the fire warden shouts a warning and staff evacuate the same way.
**Records:** Fire drill records, including the date, time, evacuation time, and any problems found, kept in the fire safety folder for three years.
## SOP 10 — End-of-day closing and handover
**Purpose:** To close safely at the end of the day, to hand over clearly to the next morning's staff, and to leave the building clean, tidy, and secure.
**Scope:** This procedure covers the end of the day, from the last collection at 6:00 pm until the building is locked, including cleaning, checks, and the written handover.
**Who does it:** The closing member of staff, who stays until every child has been collected, leads the closing check. Room leaders tidy their rooms and write the handover notes.
### Steps
1. As each child is collected, sign them out, give the parent a short handover, and check the child has all their belongings.
2. Once the last child has left, check every room to make sure no child is still in the building, including the sleep room, toilets, and outdoor area.
3. Tidy each room: put toys away, stack chairs, wipe tables, and put books back in place. Leave the rooms ready for the morning.
4. Clean the toilets, sinks, and changing area: wipe surfaces, empty bins, replace paper towels and toilet paper, and mop if needed.
5. Clean the kitchen: wash up or load the dishwasher, wipe worktops, put food away, empty the bins, and check the fridge temperature.
6. Check that all medicines are back in the locked box, cleaning products are locked away, and the first-aid box is in its place.
7. Write the handover note: any child who was unwell, any accident, any medicine given, any parent message, and anything that needs fixing.
8. Leave the handover note, the sign-in and sign-out books, and the daily sheets on the manager's desk, so morning staff can find them easily.
9. Switch off lights, heaters, and equipment, except the fridge and security system. Close and lock all windows, then lock the front and back doors.
10. Set the alarm, if one is fitted, lock the gate to the outdoor area, and check the building once more from outside before leaving.
**Quality check:** The manager reads the handover note first thing the next morning and follows up anything that needs action. The opening staff check the rooms were left tidy.
**If it goes wrong:** If a child has not been collected by closing time, stay with the child, keep trying the parent and emergency contacts, and do not leave until the child has been collected or the manager has made other arrangements. If something is broken, write it in the handover note and tell the manager by phone if it cannot wait.
**Records:** Daily sign-out book, daily handover note, and cleaning checklist, kept in the office for one year.
