Making a simple medical device is one thing. Proving you have a proper quality system behind it is another — and that is exactly what hospitals, distributors, and regulators ask about first. The ISO 13485 Quality Kit gives a very small manufacturer ten complete, ready-to-use procedures that cover the whole quality system, from the first customer complaint to the yearly internal audit.

Nothing is left as a blank template. Every procedure is written out in full, in plain English, using one invented example business all the way through: Medivance Devices Ltd, a five-person company making a reusable stainless-steel instrument tray. You can follow the example as it stands, or swap in your own company name, product, and job titles — the steps still work.

The kit starts where most small makers feel the pressure first: complaints. It shows how to log a complaint the same day, answer the customer within two working days, investigate the batch, and close the case properly. Then it shows how to look at complaints over three months and a full year, so a repeating fault — rust spots, a lid that will not close, a sharp edge — is spotted and fixed before it becomes a serious problem.

From there it covers the duties many small makers find hardest to explain: vigilance, which means reporting serious incidents to the regulator on time; post-market surveillance, which means keeping watch on your device once customers are using it; and field safety corrective action, which means recalling, repairing, or replacing devices that are already out in the world. The kit also covers the day-to-day backbone of a quality system: CAPA, which is the formal way of fixing a problem for good by finding its real root cause; document control, so nobody on the shop floor uses an old version by mistake; supplier checks, so the steel and services you buy are good enough for a medical device; internal audit, so you find your own gaps before a customer or a certification body does; and training records, so you can prove every person doing a job was trained to do it. Each procedure follows the same clear pattern — Purpose, Scope, Who does it, numbered Steps, Quality check, If it goes wrong, and Records — with realistic details, deadlines, and example numbers throughout.

The kit is written for a small team, not a big factory: the roles are the ones a five-person business actually has, and the checks are ones a small team can really do. These templates help a small manufacturer build its quality system. They are not a guarantee of ISO 13485 certification, and the buyer should get a quality consultant for certification.
